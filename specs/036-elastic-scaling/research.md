# Research & Decisions: Elastic scaling driven by telemetry

Numbering: decisions `D1700`–`D1734`, gaps `G1700`–`G1718`, owner question `O1710` (O1700–O1709 are in the specification). The ranges were checked against `origin/*` and the sibling worktrees on 2026-10-28 (highest taken before: D1671, G1669, O1599, ADR-0071, migration `20261225000000`). Design records: ADR-0072, ADR-0073, ADR-0074 (proposed).

## Decisions

- **D1700 — The scaling model and `evaluate` are a pure module of the domain crate.** Types, validation, the decision function, the role catalogue and the rank-based partition targets live in `crates/domain/src/scaling/`, using only `thiserror`, `time`, `uuid` and `zeroize`; time and any counter come in as arguments. Alternatives: the logic in the scheduler crate (cannot be tested deterministically without a database and breaks FR-036-027), or in the evaluator's adapter (two copies of the rules for the dry evaluation and the loop). `infra-signals` is an adapter by its `infra-` prefix in `scripts/check-architecture.py` (`layer_of`), so the check needs no change and enforces its layer edges. Enforced by the architecture check and by property tests.
- **D1701 — One evaluator at a time: a lease in `engine_leases` plus a fence in the decision's transaction.** The loop takes `engine_leases('scaling-evaluator')` (the monitor's mechanism) and the transaction that records a decision first reads that row `FOR SHARE` and proceeds only if it still names this holder with an unexpired time, on the database clock. A stale evaluator is refused, never merged (FR-036-031). Alternatives: a separate deployment (another role and credential for a loop the engine already has the pattern for), a Kubernetes `Lease` (a second authority the transaction cannot ask; rejected for the same reason as in ADR-0020), advisory locks (owned by a connection).
- **D1702 — A decision record carries its inputs.** `scaling_decisions` is append-only (a trigger refuses update and delete except the retention purge) and holds the policy version, the signals with value, age and use, the raw demand, the ordered limits and the outcome as bounded JSON (at most 16 signals per policy, 16 limits). That is what makes FR-036-027 true: the dry evaluation takes the recorded inputs and must give the same record. Retention 14 days by the housekeeping loop.
- **D1703 — A policy targets a deployment *unit* and names the *role* whose ceilings apply.** Units are `engine`, `control-plane`, `ingestion` (slice 2), `pool:<name>`. In slice 1 the role `INGEST` is bound to the unit `engine`, because readers live in the engine. The desired capacity of a unit is the largest final value of the policies that target it, so a scheduler policy and a lag policy cannot fight over one deployment. Ceilings are those of the *role* (the partitions bound ingestion demand, not the unit's other work).
- **D1704 — Every replica exports the desired capacity and the signal states from stored state.** The evaluator writes `scaling_desired` and `scaling_signal_state`; each replica of the engine and the control plane reads them every sampling interval and sets `scaling_desired_replicas{unit,authority}` and `scaling_signal_value{signal}`. If the evaluator is down the stored value is still exported: the role holds. Rejected: exporting under a lease and zeroing on loss (the monitor's pattern), because a zero reads as "scale in" to the provider — the exact failure FR-036-006 forbids. Unavailable signals export `NaN`, and the proof of what the provider does with it is the end-to-end test (`G1703`, `G1704`).
- **D1705 — The authority is a label, and the chart follows only `authority="authoritative"`.** Policies in *recommend* publish `advisory`, in *enforce* `authoritative` (FR-036-017). A deployment that sets nothing is unchanged because the existing `lag` and `cpu` modes remain.
- **D1706 — Configuration is one JSON document, mounted from a ConfigMap, mirrored into versioned database rows.** `SAGAT_SCALING_CONFIG_FILE` names it; it is validated against `contracts/scaling-config.schema.json`; the evaluator re-reads it when its modification time changes (each interval) and writes a version with owner `CONFIG`, author `config:<hash>`. Invalid or unreadable: the last valid version stays. **Ceilings, the destination allowlist, the database budget and the pool names are configuration-only**: the API cannot raise or widen them. Rejected: YAML (a new dependency in the engine; the chart renders JSON from values with `toJson`), database-only (no GitOps, no review), a custom resource (couples the platform to Kubernetes).
- **D1707 — Partition targets are rank-based and exact.** For P partitions and M live members sorted by identity, the first `P mod M` members get `⌊P/M⌋+1` and the rest `⌊P/M⌋`; a holder gives back its highest partitions beyond its target, and takes the lowest free ones up to it. ADR-0020's `ceil(P/M)` share cannot satisfy FR-036-007: with 12 partitions and 5 members it leaves the last joiner with none (3+3+3+3+0). The partition's position and the admission of its events stay one commit, and the position move stays fenced by the stored value (`Outcome::Superseded`), so a handover cannot lose or repeat a message beyond what the inbox deduplicates.
- **D1708 — `connector_members` is its own table, and mixed versions are safe.** The ingestion role may run without the keeper (slice 2), so connector membership cannot ride on `scheduler_members`. During a rolling upgrade an engine without the new keeper still takes free partition leases greedily; the new keeper gives back only what exceeds its target and takes only free partitions, and the fences hold. Balance is reached when the old engines are gone (`G1715`).
- **D1709 — Lag is measured where the data already is, and written one row per (connector, holder).** Kafka: the reader keeps the high watermark that `fetch_records` returns and the timestamp of the first unread record; lag is watermark − next offset, summed over the holder's partitions, no extra broker call. NATS: `Consumer::get_info()` gives `num_pending + num_ack_pending`; the age is the timestamp of the stream message after the ack floor (one extra call per sample, only when there is a backlog). SQS: `GetQueueAttributes` for visible, in-flight and delayed messages; the age is unknown (it exists only in the provider's metrics service). The sampler for NATS and SQS is whoever holds a `connector_leases` row named `~lag`. A row carries `partitions_covered` and `partitions_total`; the aggregate has a **coverage**, and a signal below full coverage permits scale-out and never scale-in (`D1720`).
- **D1710 — The rate limit is already global; the drain state says when it is the cause.** Found at planning (`Evidence` above). `Admission::RateLimited { retry_after_secs }` stops being folded into `Backlogged`: `Outcome` gains `RateLimited { retry_after }`, and the readers (which already sleep 5 s) use the time they are given. The connector reports `RATE_LIMITED` with the retry time, `BLOCKED` for the tenant's undispatched-event limit. The runbook sentence "limit per replica" is wrong and is corrected; the spec's FR-036-013 and O1705 were corrected the same day. No behaviour changes.
- **D1711 — Scale-out for lag ignores connectors that more readers cannot drain.** The ingestion signals sum only connectors in `BEHIND` or `CAUGHT_UP`. The ceiling of useful readers is the largest partition count among the drainable Kafka connectors with a backlog, and unbounded when a drainable NATS or SQS connector has one (`G1701`); both are recorded as inputs of the decision, and a clamp by it is recorded as `PARTITION_BOUND`.
- **D1712 — Reader parallelism changes by a channel, not a restart; it is bounded by a new quota `connector_parallelism` whose default is a platform cap.** (Revised 2026-10-28: the first version had a cap and no quota; the owner's answer to O1703 says "within its quota", so the plan follows it.) The supervisor restarts a reader when `updated_at` changes; for a change that touches only parallelism or rate it sends the new value on a `watch` channel instead, so Kafka leases are not released and NATS messages are not redelivered. Parallelism defaults to 1 (today's serial reading). The quota `connector_parallelism` is per namespace, with a default and ceiling equal to `SAGAT_CONNECTOR_MAX_PARALLELISM`, and follows the pattern of `iteration_concurrency`; the rate a tenant sets is already bounded by the namespace's `events_per_second` quota. The cost is about ten files: `domain/quota.rs` (and `ALL.len()`), `application/quotas.rs`, `infra-postgres/quotas.rs`, a migration that rewrites the CHECK of the quota tables, `storage-classification.json`, the OpenAPI `QuotaResourceName` enum, the Studio `schema.d.ts`, the runbooks `security-governance.md` and `scaling.md`, and the tests of `tests/integration/security-multitenancy/quotas.rs` and the REST conformance suite. It lands in M6 as a third migration, `20261226000200`.
- **D1713 — The operator surface is a separate listener with its own principal (default of O1710).** `SAGAT_OPERATOR_ADDR` (off by default), verified bearer tokens with a separate audience (`sagat-operator`) through the existing token verification, producing a `PlatformOperator` principal that only that listener's extractor can create. Permissions `scaling:read`, `scaling:manage`, `scaling:override`, `scaling:publish`, each operation requiring exactly one (FR-036-023). The interface is absent unless enabled and its peers are explicit (FR-036-063). Alternatives are in O1710. See ADR-0074, D1732 to D1734.
- **D1732 — The evaluator re-validates every definition at each evaluation.** Policies, sources and custom signals are checked against the configuration-only ceilings, the database budget, the allowlist and the pool names every round, not only when written (FR-036-064). A version that fails is refused with a condition and the unit holds. This is what keeps rows written by a compromised runtime role (`G1707`) inside the bounds: it can write the table, and cannot make the evaluator act on a violation. Tested by writing violating rows directly (`SC-036-021`).
- **D1733 — Operator tokens are short-lived and rate limited.** The operator listener refuses a token whose lifetime exceeds one hour, records the subject and the token identifier on every action, and limits each token to 20 requests per second (configurable) with a typed refusal; revocation is expiry and rotation of the verification keys of the operator audience (FR-036-062, FR-036-068). The tenant endpoints stay under the namespace's request-rate quota.
- **D1734 — Audit actors are fixed.** An operator's change records the token subject and identifier; a change from the configuration file records `configuration` and the document digest with the version's reason; a decision applied in *enforce* records `scaling-evaluator`. Refused attempts are audited without the request content and counted on `scaling_refused_total{interface,reason}` (FR-036-065, FR-036-066).
- **D1714 — Platform-scope changes have their own append-only audit trail.** `AuditRecord` requires a tenant and a placeholder tenant would receive quota provisioning (`tenants_quota_usage`) and appear in listings. `platform_audit` records actor, action, resource, reason, a digest of the before and after, trace id and time, and the operator listener writes it in the same transaction as the change. Tenant-scope changes (connector consumption) use the tenant audit as today.
- **D1715 — Contracts: a separate OpenAPI document for the operator listener; a fragment for the tenant endpoints.** The operator document is not part of `openapi.yaml` (a different listener and audience). The tenant fragment is merged into `specs/001-platform-foundation/contracts/openapi.yaml` with `x-implemented: '036'` so that the per-feature conformance test requires a scenario for each operation; Redocly lints both; `docs/reference/api.md` and the Studio `schema.d.ts` are regenerated after reading the diff. `sagat scaling` is a client of the operator listener and never writes the database.
- **D1716 — Pulled sources: two kinds, one guard.** `METRICS_QUERY` (an instant query to a Prometheus-compatible endpoint, reduced to one finite number) and `BROKER_QUEUE` (the lag probes of `D1709` against a declared queue, topic or stream). Both go through `OperatorGuard`, an implementation of the `infra-egress` `Guard` seam: only destinations on the configuration allowlist, an address literal checked up front, every resolved address checked by the connector's resolver, redirects never followed, no proxy, 5 s timeout, 64 KiB response, a minimum interval, a retry with backoff, and a read-only operation by construction (the evaluator builds the request; the operator supplies the query text only). The tenant egress policy refuses private addresses by design, so it cannot be reused for an in-cluster monitoring system; the operator allowlist can allow one by name and port.
- **D1717 — Source credentials are file names.** A declaration holds the name of a file under a directory the chart mounts from Kubernetes Secrets (`/etc/sagat/scaling/secrets/<name>`); the file is read at use and never stored, logged or returned. The API cannot introduce a secret that the deployment did not mount. A source with a credential must use TLS and is refused otherwise (the rule connectors already apply). OpenBao is not used here: operator sources are not in a tenant namespace, and the existing reader is namespace-scoped.
- **D1718 — Custom signals are published by operators only in slice 1.** A tenant cannot publish (`G1700`). An operator's sample has a declared range and a time to live; out of range is refused at publication, stale after the time to live.
- **D1719 — The order of limits puts the safety ceilings last.** See `contracts/evaluation.md`. An override changes the bounds or holds the value, and then the role ceiling, the database budget and the cost ceiling still apply, so no override, freeze release or bad signal can pass them.
- **D1720 — Hysteresis and coverage.** *Utilisation* has a tolerance band (10 % by default) inside which the ratio counts as 1; *demand* has none (it is a step function of ceil). A signal with coverage below 1 is a lower bound: it may raise the demand and may never lower the capacity.
- **D1721 — Identical consecutive decisions coalesce.** Recording "held, nothing changed" every 15 s per policy is 5,760 rows a day per policy. A decision equal to the previous one in outcome, final value and reasons updates `last_seen_at` and `repeat_count` of that row instead of inserting.
- **D1722 — The database budget is checked by the decision function.** `share(unit) = ⌊(server_max − reserve) × weight(unit) ÷ Σ weight⌋` from configuration; the final replicas are limited so that `replicas × per_replica_budget ≤ share`. In slice 1 the per-replica budget is the configured static value; making it a tunable is slice 3 (`G1712`).
- **D1723 — New metrics, bounded labels.** `contracts/metrics.md`. Labels are `unit`, `signal`, `role`, `limit`, `outcome`, `authority`, `kind`, `state`, none on the forbidden list; their values are bounded by validation (at most 64 signals, 32 policies, the fixed unit and limit names, operator-named pools). Connector lag is exported only aggregated by `kind`.
- **D1724 — Two additive migrations from `20261226000000`.** `scaling_core` (policies and versions, sources, signals, custom samples, overrides, controls, desired, signal state, decisions, platform audit) and `connector_lag_members` (members, lag rows, drain columns on `event_connectors`). Grants for `sagat_app` only where the code needs them; `engine_leases` already exists; storage classification for every variable-size column; down migrations refuse data loss. Before taking the timestamp the implementer greps `origin/*` again.
- **D1725 — The chart gets a ConfigMap, an environment variable, a `platform` autoscaling mode and explicit egress.** `engine.autoscaling.mode: platform` renders one KEDA trigger on `max(scaling_desired_replicas{unit="engine",authority="authoritative"})` with KEDA's own stabilization neutral (the platform already stabilizes, `G1711`); `scaling.*` values render the ConfigMap; sources require matching `networkPolicy.egress.engineExtra` peers and the render refuses a source whose destination is not allowed; the render refuses `platform` with the other two modes. The contract test is extended, not loosened.
- **D1726 — The evaluator reuses the monitor's sampler for the signals that exist.** Scheduler lateness, outbox and pool queues come from the same `sample()` the monitor uses (no second set of queries); the pending-delivery oldest age is one new query; connector lag comes from `connector_lag`. The monitor's own gauges and its zero-on-failure behaviour are unchanged.
- **D1727 — All times are the database's.** Leases, sample times and decision times use `clock_timestamp()`; the evaluator takes `now` once per round and passes it to `evaluate`. A monotonic local clock is used only to stop acting after a lease would have expired, as the keeper does (ADR-0020, D232).
- **D1728 — Test strategy.** `proptest` for the decision function (never above the ceiling, below the floor, over the budget, past the step, on a stale signal); real PostgreSQL and real brokers for lag, assignment and handover; the held-lock pattern for two evaluators; the chart rendered by `helm template`; kind, Cilium and KEDA for the provider loop. Results are reported as they are, failing or skipped included.
- **D1729 — Documentation corrected in the same delivery.** `docs/runbooks/events.md` (rate "per replica"), `docs/runbooks/scaling.md` (a stale line says KEDA is not exercised, `G1714`), and new sections for the new alerts.
- **D1730 — A requirement that serves several stories is delivered with the first of them** (O1709). The mapping is the table in `plan.md`; `tasks.md` carries it per task.
- **D1731 — The drain state is written by the existing once-a-second progress flush.** `event_connectors` gains `drain_state`, `drain_state_at` and `rate_retry_at`, updated by the statement that already updates the counters. Blocked and rate-limited are tenant-wide conditions (one bucket, one limit), so every reader sees the same state and the last writer is right; *failing* is per reader (`G1709`).

## Gaps

- **G1700 — Tenants cannot publish custom signals in slice 1.** It needs a permission (the identity domain has 48) and a quota; with O1703 a tenant's signal has no automatic effect anyway.
- **G1701 — The ingestion ceiling is approximate when connector kinds are mixed.** A drainable NATS or SQS connector makes it unbounded, even if the replicas that exceed the Kafka partitions would idle for the Kafka connector.
- **G1702 — SQS lag is approximate and has no age, and needs `sqs:GetQueueAttributes`.** The runbook says so; the counts are the provider's eventually consistent estimates.
- **G1703 — What the provider does with an absent or `NaN` series is proved only by the end-to-end test.** It is not assumed.
- **G1704 — The metrics facade keeps a series' last value for 600 s of silence.** An unavailable signal therefore exports `NaN` explicitly instead of going quiet.
- **G1705 — Kafka lag has partial coverage while a partition has no holder.** The signal is a lower bound and forbids scale-in.
- **G1706 — The operator listener is a new attack surface.** Off by default, separate audience, explicit network peers.
- **G1707 — The runtime role writes the scaling tables, as it does the lease tables (feature 010, G259).** A compromised control plane could rewrite policies, but only inside the configuration-only ceilings, allowlist and budget.
- **G1708 — The configuration reload is a modification-time poll,** and a ConfigMap reaches a pod after a delay Kubernetes decides.
- **G1709 — Last-writer drain state can hide a single failing reader** among healthy ones.
- **G1710 — The division of the event dispatcher and the delivery worker, and the claim path, are slice 2 and depend on the benchmark** (features 010, G243 and G246).
- **G1711 — The provider's own stabilization and cooldown add to the platform's.** The chart sets the provider's neutral in `platform` mode; whether that is enough is part of the end-to-end test.
- **G1712 — A connection-budget tunable meets a pool fixed at creation.** Slice 3 decides between a permit semaphore under a hard cap and a rebuilt pool.
- **G1713 — No Studio view of signals, policies and decisions** (out of scope of the spec); the API and the CLI are the surface.
- **G1714 — `docs/runbooks/scaling.md:102` says KEDA is not exercised, while G247 is resolved.** Corrected here.
- **G1715 — Mixed-version behaviour is tested with an old and a new keeper in one process,** not with two released binaries (as feature 010's G255).
- **G1716 — Policies are per unit and role, not per connector.** A tenant's lag can raise a shared unit; the cost ceiling and the step bound it, and the operator-only record flags dominance.
- **G1717 — The platform audit trail is read through the API and the CLI only.**
- **G1718 — Credential-in-text detection is a heuristic.** The declaration validator refuses user information in a destination and parameters named like a credential in the query string or the query text (FR-036-058); it cannot recognise a secret written as free text, and says so.

## Owner questions

- **O1710 — How does an operator act through the management interface?** The REST API knows only tenant principals; today's operator actions are owner-role commands (`quota-ceiling`, `scheduling-weight`) because "no API call does" raise a ceiling (`security-governance.md`). The specification asks for programmatic operator management (FR-036-019, FR-036-023). Options:
  1. **A separate operator listener and principal** (the plan's default): off by default, a separate audience, only that listener creates the principal, ceilings stay configuration-only, `sagat scaling` is its client. Costs a second OpenAPI document and a new authority.
  2. **An operator role on the tenant API**: one listener, but a cross-tenant principal on the surface tenants reach, and a change to the identity pipeline of feature 009.
  3. **Owner-role commands only** (the `control-plane` pattern) with read-only REST: no new authority, but no programmatic write for an operator, which contradicts FR-036-019 and the answer to O1708.
  *Recommended:* option 1. Until the owner answers, the plan and ADR-0074 assume it; M6 is where it lands.
  *What changes with another answer.* Option 2: FR-036-062 and FR-036-063 collapse to one listener, the identity pipeline of feature 009 gains a platform principal, the second OpenAPI document becomes a tag of the first, and M6 grows with a security review of the tenant door. Option 3: FR-036-019 and US05 narrow to the tenant endpoints plus a read-only operator API, the operator OpenAPI loses its write operations, and policies are written by the configuration file and by `control-plane` commands with the owner role.
