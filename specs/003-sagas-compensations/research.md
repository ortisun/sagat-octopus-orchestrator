# Research & Decisions: Native sagas and compensations

## Main decision

SagaCoordinator as a journal reducer in the application domain; saga_entries table and compensations immutable per version; reverse DAG policies.

## Rationale

Orchestrate long-running business transactions, with compensations, controlled reprocessing and reconciliation. The decision respects the recovery, auditability, security and versioning properties defined in the Constitution.

## Alternatives considered

1. **Generic ad hoc queue-based solution:** rejected as the sole durability mechanism, since message delivery is not equivalent to a transaction over workflow state.
2. **Independent services per feature from day 1:** postponed to avoid operational and consistency complexity without a benchmark.
3. **SDK-specific logic:** rejected for core invariants, to avoid behavior differences between languages.

## Risks and mitigation

- Complexity/scope risk: deliver P1 user stories separately, running contract tests.
- Semantic ambiguity risk: encode reducers and golden fixtures, do not decide via UI alone.
- Crash/redelivery risk: test harness with real failures at checkpoints.
- Security risk: specific pentest and tenant isolation from the first milestone.

## Questions for spike validation

- Latency and overhead benchmarks on the critical path defined for this feature.
- Detail compliance and retention requirements according to the deployment environment.

## Decisions taken in this feature

- **D59 — A compensation and a lookup are steps of the definition, derived when it is parsed, not a second engine.** For every activity with `compensateWith` the definition gets a node `<id>~c`, and for every one with `reconcileWith` a node `<id>~r`. The character `~` is outside the alphabet of the ids an author may write (`NodeId::parse` refuses it; `parse_stored` reads it back), so a derived node cannot collide with a declared one, nothing routes to it, and the database refuses a step whose `kind` does not match its id. They are therefore leased, fenced, retried, timed out, journaled, projected and listed in the graph by the machinery that already does that for forward work, with the same crash and duplicate guarantees. The cost is that the graph has nodes the author did not write; they are marked `kind` and `forward`, and a workflow without a saga has none.
- **D60 — A saga is a closed region with a coordinator that is a function of the run state.** The `saga` node names its first node (`begin`); everything reachable from it, up to a node that has no `next`, is its body (a body may not contain an `end` node, may not be entered from outside, and sagas nest, at most 32 per workflow). The saga step waits (`WAITING`, `waiting on saga`) while its body runs. The coordinator moves it through `FORWARD → ABORTING → COMPENSATING → DONE` by emitting journal events from `advance(def, state, stop)`: it looks at the state and says the next thing that has to happen, so the same function drives a worker report, a lease that ran out, a timer, a cancellation and a restart, and a run replayed from its journal arrives at the same place. An unhandled failure in the body (no `onError` route inside the region) or a stop of the run aborts the saga; a failure that something inside handles does not.
- **D61 — The plan is the reverse of the dependencies, and is recorded with the version.** A compensation waits for the compensations of the *nearest* entries downstream of it, passing through nodes that have nothing to undo, so a chain of N stores N links and not N(N−1)/2, and the branches of a `parallel` node do not wait for each other. The end of a branch waits for what came after the join (it happened later, so it is undone first); the walk visits what lies between two entries once however many entries lead into it, so a tail shared by thousands of branches is not walked thousands of times (checked against the long way on four shapes). The plan is a pure function of the definition: it is stored at publish (`saga_plans`, `saga_plan_entries`, immutable by trigger, tenant-bound by composite foreign keys) so it can be read, shown and audited without re-deriving it, and a test compares the stored plan with the derived one. The plan a *run* follows is decided when the saga stops going forward, from what actually completed: a step that never ran has nothing to undo.
- **D62 — The idempotency key of a compensation is stable per run, step and action, and the target's key travels with it.** `compensation:{compensation_id}:{run}`, where `compensation_id` is a version-8 UUID derived (SHA-256) from the workflow version and the forward node, so it is the same for every run of a version, differs between versions and between steps, and is recorded in the plan. The lookup's key is `reconciliation:{run}:{forward}`. The task also carries `target_node` and `target_idempotency_key`: the key that step's own attempts carried, which is what identifies the *effect* at the provider ("undo the payment you know as this"). Asking again after a lost response or a restart sends the same keys, so a provider that deduplicates by key does the effect once (`compensation_key` in `crates/application/src/execution.rs`; `crashes::a_crash_after_the_provider_released_and_before_the_report_releases_once`, `provider::a_release_delivered_twice...`).
- **D63 — Defaults: five attempts at undoing, ten at looking up, and a person after that.** A compensation that keeps failing is a decision for an operator, so it is given longer than a forward step: five attempts, exponential from two seconds to five minutes, with jitter (`RetryPolicy::COMPENSATION`), overridable with `compensationRetry` and `compensationTimeoutMs`. A lookup is cheap and the alternative to an answer is a person, so it gets ten attempts shared by every round of looking up one step, exponential from one to thirty seconds (`RetryPolicy::RECONCILIATION`). When the attempts of a compensation are spent the saga does not fail: with `onCompensationFailure: manual_review` (the default) the step waits for an operator and the run is `MANUAL_REVIEW`; with `fail` the saga ends `COMPENSATION_FAILED`.
- **D64 — An ambiguous attempt is looked up before anything else is decided; a step that cannot be looked up is not retried blindly if it is high risk.** An attempt that ends without a known outcome — the lease ran out (`LEASE_EXPIRED`), the attempt timed out (`ATTEMPT_TIMEOUT`) or the worker said `OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` (a reserved code, non-retryable) — makes the step wait and schedules its `x~r`. The worker answers `{"effect": "applied" | "not_applied" | "unknown", "output": …}`: *applied* completes the step (with the output the lookup found, which later steps read) without a second attempt; *not applied* sends it to its retry, or, if no attempt is left, fails it and the saga compensates; *unknown* is never assumed either way and goes to an operator. Without `reconcileWith`, an ordinary step is retried as before this feature; a `highRisk` one waits for an operator instead of being retried. A step whose outcome stayed unknown when its saga compensates is compensated *defensively* (G53). An ordinary failure — the worker says it did not happen — is not looked up.
- **D65 — A lookup that answers nonsense fails for good, and an answer that would make the run keep too much is refused.** Asking the same lookup again would get the same answer, so a body that is not `{"effect": …}` fails `x~r` with `RECONCILIATION_ANSWER_INVALID` without retry and the step goes to an operator (the report itself is acknowledged). An *applied* output counts against the 4 MiB a run may retain like any output (`RUN_DATA_LIMIT`). A lookup is completed with its verdict and nothing else (`ExecutionError::NeedsVerdict`).
- **D66 — The statuses, and what a stop does to a saga.** A run is `COMPENSATING` while a saga undoes its work and `MANUAL_REVIEW` while a step waits for a person (both live), and ends `COMPENSATED`, `PARTIAL` (something could not be undone: an `irreversible` step, a compensation an operator skipped, an unknown outcome with nothing that can undo it) or `COMPENSATION_FAILED`; a cancellation or a deadline ends it `CANCELLED` or `TIMED_OUT` *only when the compensation is done*, and `PARTIAL` or `COMPENSATION_FAILED` if the undoing was not complete. The cancel policy of the saga decides what a stop does: `compensate_completed` (default) stops what is in flight and undoes what completed; `wait_in_flight_then_compensate` lets the attempts in flight finish and then undoes everything that completed; `abort_no_compensation` just stops. A compensation runs although its run is being stopped, and a stop supersedes a pause (a paused run that is cancelled is no longer paused, or nothing could be leased to undo its work). Who is given up on depends on who ended the run: when the run is **cancelled or passes its deadline** nobody is left to decide, so when the stop is what *started* the abort the people nobody can wait for are given up on: a forward step that waits for an operator is cancelled (and compensated defensively); a *compensation* that fails because of the stop waits for an operator like any other, because the person is the only one who can say that the reservation stays or is released. When the run ends because **one of its own branches failed** (a sibling saga compensated, a plain step failed) the people the other sagas ask are still the only ones who can decide, so nothing is given up on and the run waits. The run's final status is the worst of the stop's and of every saga that ended while the run was ending, so a sibling saga whose compensation failed is never hidden by the one that ended the run. A saga's `onError` route is for a failure that was *undone*: if the outcome is `PARTIAL` or `COMPENSATION_FAILED` the failure is unhandled and the run ends with that status instead of carrying on as if nothing had happened. A child run that ends `COMPENSATED`, `PARTIAL` or `COMPENSATION_FAILED` fails its parent's step with `CHILD_COMPENSATED`, `CHILD_PARTIAL` or `CHILD_COMPENSATION_FAILED`. (A first version used one flag for both kinds of stop and left a cancelled run in `MANUAL_REVIEW` for ever; found by `stopping::cancelling_a_run_that_waits_for_an_operator...`, fixed, and covered by a domain test and a mutation check. The rest of this paragraph was found by the independent review, D71.)
- **D67 — The operator decides through the operations route, with a reason, and the permission depends on what is decided.** `POST /runs/{id}/operations` gains `RETRY_STEP` / `RETRY_COMPENSATION` (another attempt; `run:operate`, so an Operator can), `SKIP_COMPENSATION` (give up on a compensation, the saga ends `PARTIAL`) and `RESOLVE_STEP` (declare that the step happened or did not, with `outcome` `SUCCEEDED` or `FAILED` and an `errorCode`), both of which need `saga:override`, which only an administrator has. Asking again for a *forward* step whose outcome may have happened (it waits with `LEASE_EXPIRED`, `ATTEMPT_TIMEOUT`, `OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` or a lookup that could not say) is the decision the platform would not take by itself, so it needs `saga:override` too; an operator may retry a compensation (idempotent by contract) or a step known not to have happened. A retry is refused (409) on a step of a saga that stopped going forward, where it would be cancelled the moment it was granted. Each needs `stepId` (the node id: `reserve~c`), a justification of 8 to 2000 characters **after trimming** (enforced by the API and by the use case), optionally `expectedRunVersion`, and an `Idempotency-Key`; each is audited with the reason, in full, in the transaction that records it (`saga.step_retried`, `saga.step_skipped`, `saga.step_resolved`, the last as `declared SUCCEEDED: …` or `declared FAILED(CODE): …`), and the first of two racing decisions wins while the second is a 409 that writes nothing. The journal keeps the old attempts and the request for a decision: nothing is erased.
- **D68 — A circuit breaker keeps a provider that is down from being asked by every run at once.** Per tenant, **namespace** and activity name (`compensation_breakers`; a namespace is an environment, and a worker that fails in `dev` must not hold back the same activity in `prod`): after `SAGAT_COMPENSATION_BREAKER_THRESHOLD` (5) attempts of compensations in a row that ended without success the breaker opens for `SAGAT_COMPENSATION_BREAKER_OPEN_SECS` (30); when the window is over exactly one caller wins an `UPDATE` and its lease is the *probe*, whose success deletes the row and whose failure reopens it for twice as long (the level counts failed probes), up to `SAGAT_COMPENSATION_BREAKER_MAX_OPEN_SECS` (300). A failure that arrives while it is open belongs to an attempt that was already running when it opened: it is counted and changes nothing else, so a burst of them cannot walk the window to its ceiling without a single probe. The rules are a pure function (`breaker::State::failed`, unit-tested and mutation-checked). It is checked where the lease is granted (one indexed read, on the transaction's own connection) and in the query that picks candidates: compensations have an index of their own, and the branch that reads it is gated by a condition on the breaker alone, so with the breaker open the planner does not enter it (`EXPLAIN` shows `One-Time Filter` and `never executed`; tested) and the compensations an outage left `READY` are not walked by the claim of anything else. A compensation the breaker holds back does not use up the place of one that can run. Outcomes are recorded in the transaction that records the attempt's end, so a crash cannot count one without the other. Operator decisions and forward work never count; a forward step whose activity has the name of a held-back compensation is not held back (the predicate is on the step's kind). Threshold `0` turns it off. G54, G55, G56.
- **D69 — The counters are taken after the commit and carry closed labels.** `saga_transitions_total{event}`, `saga_outcomes_total{outcome}`, `compensation_attempts_total{result}`, `reconciliations_total{stage}`, `operator_decisions_total{action}` and `compensation_breaker_opened_total` are emitted by `PgRunTx::commit` from the events the transaction persisted, so a rolled-back transition leaves no trace and a crash between commit and count loses at most a count, never state (the journal is the record). No label is a tenant, run, node or payment (checked by `telemetry::compensations_reconciliations_and_decisions_are_counted_without_naming_anyone`).
- **D70 — The contracts only grow.** `worker.proto` gains `task_kind`, `target_node` and `target_idempotency_key` on `TaskLease` (protocol 1.2; a worker that ignores them runs a compensation as the ordinary activity it names, and an empty kind is `FORWARD`; the compatibility baseline gained 15 lines and lost none). The OpenAPI document gains `getCompensationPlans`, the run statuses, `SKIPPED`, the waiting reasons, the `kind`, `forward` and `saga` fields and `compensation` / `reconcile` edges of the runtime graph, and the two new actions and their fields; the workflow schema gains `saga`, `compensateWith`, `reconcileWith`, `compensationRetry`, `compensationTimeoutMs`, `irreversible` and `highRisk`. A run started before this feature has no derived nodes and nothing changes for it.
- **D71 — Two independent reviews, and what they found.** Before this feature was considered done, two reviewers read the uncommitted change with no part in writing it (one for security and tenant isolation, one for correctness and crash safety). Neither found a tenant-isolation break or a divergence between the journal and its projections. They found, and every one was reproduced by a test that failed before the fix and passes after it, then mutation-checked: (1) a parent whose child ended `COMPENSATED`, `PARTIAL` or `COMPENSATION_FAILED` waited for ever, and such children sat at the head of the list of ended children and starved the others (the scheduler now maps every status, exhaustively); (2) a run could end `COMPENSATED` while a sibling saga's compensation failed, and that sibling's review was cancelled automatically (D66); (3) cancelling a paused run left its compensations unleasable (D66); (4) the plan did not order what ran after a join before the branches that fed it (D61); (5) an attempt that was lost while the run was being cancelled was not undone, although the same loss a moment earlier was (the cancelled step now keeps the code); (6) a saga's `onError` route let a run succeed after a compensation failed or was skipped (D66); (7) after a lookup said *not applied* the step still looked like an unknown outcome (it now carries `RECONCILED_NOT_APPLIED`); (8) a compensation that worked could be recorded as failed because the run's retained outputs were full (G65); (9) an operator retry on a step of a saga that was winding down was cancelled at once (refused, D67); (10) a compensation held back by the breaker used up the place of one that could run, and the claim query walked every held-back compensation (D68); (11) the plan was computed in quadratic time and again on every graph request (D61); (12) a justification of one letter and seven spaces passed the length check, and what was audited was cut to 512 characters (D67); (13) a window that doubled per failure instead of per probe, and a breaker shared by every namespace of a tenant (D68); (14) a blind retry of a high-risk step with an unknown outcome needed only `run:operate` (D67); (15) the audit of a settled step did not say what was declared (D67); (16) the `run.finished` event contract did not list the new statuses (found by the author while documenting; the contract and a test now cover it). What was not changed, and why, is G62–G65.
