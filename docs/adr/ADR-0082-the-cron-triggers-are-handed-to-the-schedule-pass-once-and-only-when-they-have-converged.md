# ADR-0082: The cron triggers are handed to the schedule pass once, and only when their schedules have converged

**Status:** Proposed (2026-11-08), awaiting the owner's acceptance
**Decided by:** agent (tasks T018-060, T018-061), after the correctness, security and architecture reviews of 2026-11-08 and the third round of them; the owner has not accepted it.
**Refines / supersedes:** refines ADR-0081 (the first feature to activate through the registry) and ADR-0050 decision 9; **narrows ADR-0081 decision 2** (see below); amends `plan.md` of feature 018 ("the migration creates one trigger-owned schedule per existing cron trigger").
**Enforced by:** only checks that exist and run. `tests/integration/schedules-bulk-operations/trigger_round3.rs` (one definition of converged over every state, the cron that never fires, the pause in `LEGACY`, the order and the bound of the locks derived from `pg_locks` and a long publication, the state the function does not know, the name of the operator, the command as the binary runs it, the legacy pass held across the handover, the unreadable state), `trigger_followup.rs` (the refusal under the lock with a publication in flight, the removal guard, the control window, the lock-order deadlock test on the real code, the tripwire), `trigger_handover.rs` (owner-only, one-way, the command line), `trigger_scale.rs` (the window and the sweep over 20,000 triggers), `trigger_schedules.rs` (quota exemption, publication), `tests/fault/schedules-bulk-operations/upgrade.rs` (one run per tick across the rolling upgrade), the privilege audit `tests/integration/platform-foundation/runtime_privileges.rs` for the owner-only functions, and `contract_observability_operations` for the alerts and the runbook sections they point to. There is **no** rule of `scripts/check-architecture.py` for the handover: the architecture enforcement of this decision is the tests above. The alert rule tests exist and are not yet run by CI (see Consequences).

## Context

Feature 004 fires cron triggers from the engines' legacy pass. Feature 018 makes each trigger a schedule so that an operator can pause it, and hands the firing over to the schedule pass at an operator's command. Four properties of that handover are hard to reverse or surprising without their reasons: how the schedules of the existing triggers come to exist, whether they use the tenant's schedule quota, what authority the engine has when it starts a tick, and what exactly the one-way switch requires.

## Decision

1. **No data migration makes the schedules of the existing triggers (D2033).** Publication makes the schedule of each trigger it registers, in the publishing transaction, and a reconciliation pass in every engine that runs the schedule pass makes the rest (a control plane older than the feature, triggers older than the feature). A migration cannot plan a next time (the calendar and the time-zone database are not in the schema) and would walk every tenant's rows while holding locks.
2. **Trigger-owned schedules are exempt from the `schedules` quota (D2034).** The quota is the tenant's to spend on schedules it makes; the number of triggers is bounded per workflow (16) by the workflow's own limits. A publication fails at the tenant content ceiling (the schedule and revision rows are charged to it) as it already does for the version itself; it is never refused for the schedule quota.
3. **The engine is the authority of a trigger-owned schedule and needs no membership (D2039, D2046).** `system:engine` holds no membership; the admission of a run whose schedule has a trigger and whose authority is the engine does not look one up, in every path that starts it (the tick, the release of an action that waited, a run-now whose waiting action was released). A person's backfill or run-now keeps the person's authority check. Database steps stand on the publisher, as they do for the legacy pass.
4. **"Converged" has one definition, and the handover is one-way and requires it (D2046, D2048, D2069).** The view `schedule_trigger_drift` lists the cron triggers whose schedule is not equal to them (none, a tombstone, another expression or zone, not planned, or paused while the state is `LEGACY`); the reconciliation repairs what the view lists and the handover counts it, so they cannot disagree. A calendar with no occurrence is marked exhausted and is converged. `schedule_trigger_handover_complete` is owner-only; under the requirement epoch it takes a lock on the triggers' table (no publication commits meanwhile), asks whether the view is empty and refuses unless it is; it then moves the state (only from `LEGACY`), records the operator and `session_user`, and the requirement of `schedules-v1` can never be removed again while the state is `HANDED_OVER`. There is no command to go back.
5. **The locks are taken in one order and held briefly (D2070, D2071).** The requirement epoch, the table of the cron triggers, the handover row. The table lock waits at most two seconds (`HANDOVER_BUSY`, "run the command again", nothing recorded), so that the most a writer stalls because of the command is two seconds. Under the lock the check is an `EXISTS`; the count and the ids are read outside it.
6. **The control of a trigger-owned schedule opens a few seconds after the commit (D2053, D2072).** Engines believe `LEGACY` for at most 2 s (and read the state before each trigger, not once per round); control (pause, resume, run now, backfill) is refused until `changed_at` plus 5 s by the database's clock; a unit test ties the two constants with a margin of two seconds.
7. **A round costs a window, not the number of triggers (D2072).** The reconciliation reads 2,000 triggers at a time in the order of an index, and sweeps; its writes are paced at 100 a second.

## Narrows ADR-0081 decision 2

ADR-0081 says that the feature's step of an activation "MUST NOT take a lock that a transaction reading the epoch may hold: it reads, it does not lock". The step of this handover **does lock**: `LOCK TABLE workflow_triggers IN SHARE ROW EXCLUSIVE MODE` (bounded at two seconds) and `FOR UPDATE` on the singleton handover row. The reason is the guarantee the handover exists for: no publication of a cron trigger may commit between the count of the triggers that have no schedule and the commit of the state, or the trigger would be published after the legacy pass stopped and before the schedule pass knew of it, and nothing would fire it. The census alone cannot give that, because a publication in flight is invisible to a count. The narrowing is safe for ADR-0081's reason: **no transaction that reads the epoch (the heartbeat, a registration, `platform_requirement_lock` readers) touches `workflow_triggers` at table level or the handover row**; the only transactions that take those locks are this activation and publications and the legacy pass's moves of a trigger, and none of them holds the epoch, so none can wait for the activation while holding what the activation wants. A publication that holds the table or a row can delay the activation, never the other way round beyond the two-second bound. ADR-0081 decision 2 stands for every other step.

## Considered options

- *A data migration for the existing triggers.* Rejected for the reason in 1.
- *Count trigger-owned schedules against the quota.* Rejected: a tenant at its quota could not publish a cron workflow.
- *A reverse command.* Rejected: after the handover a trigger fired by the schedule pass may be paused; going back would resurrect the legacy pass that ignores a pause (G1011).
- *A precheck by the drift gauge.* Rejected: it excludes entries that are backing off, so a stuck entry reads zero (H); the census counts all shards in the activation's own transaction.
- *Reject a cron that never fires at publication with a typed error, as the only answer.* It is done (feature 004's calendar refuses it); it is not enough, because a row an older build wrote must not block the handover of the platform, so such a row is marked exhausted.
- *Fail closed when the handover state cannot be read.* Rejected (D2075): it would lose ticks on a fleet whose engines do not run the schedule pass; the failures are counted and alerted on instead.

## Consequences

An upgrade has a window of seconds in which a published trigger has no next time (the legacy pass fires meanwhile); a handover can be refused for a while after an upgrade (the operator waits for the reconciliation, which is paced at 100 writes a second and takes 20 entries of a tenant a round).

**Measured and estimated, said apart.** *Measured* (one developer machine, PostgreSQL 16 in Docker, other sessions running; `trigger_scale::a_round_costs_the_window_it_reads_and_a_sweep_finds_every_drift_over_twenty_thousand_triggers`): 20,000 triggers of 200 tenants, five with no schedule: the convergence count took 48 and 51 ms in two runs, a window of 2,000 triggers at most 63 and 66 ms, eleven windows covered every trigger (the seeding of the rows is not part of the measurement). *Measured in round 2 with the single query that the window replaced*: 100,000 triggers, drift 122 ms and count 34 ms. **Estimated, not measured:** the sweep of 100,000 triggers is 50 windows (about twelve seconds at a window every 250 ms), and a backlog of 100,000 unplanned triggers of one tenant drains in about seventeen minutes at 20 entries a round and 100 writes a second; across many tenants fewer.

Accepted limits, each written in the runbook (`docs/runbooks/schedules.md` 6.2 and 6.4) and in `research.md`:

- an old engine binary that never registers keeps its legacy pass after the handover and ignores a pause (G2005); the tripwire counts it only for ticks whose nominal time is after the handover plus ten seconds (the table keeps no real fire time), assumes a legacy round ends within ten seconds of the commit, and works only while one engine of this release runs the schedule pass (D2074, G2019);
- the census covers the execution engines only: a control plane older than the feature can publish a cron trigger after the handover, and its first ticks are lost until the reconciliation plans it; the operator attests that every control plane runs this build (D2072, G2020); a trigger published before the reconciliation runs has no next time until the next round;
- the handover has no tenant audit entry of its own; its record is the handover row and the registry's history, and the owner login is shared, so the database role recorded with a handover does not tell operators apart (D2042);
- a legacy pass that cannot read the state goes on firing (D2075);
- a backfill over ticks the legacy pass started more than seven days ago starts a second run (G2021);
- **no released build lists `schedules-v1`**: the registry commit is in no tag (`v0.1.0`, `v0.2.0`), so no build in the field can have listed it and revoked the engine's authority; a paused trigger schedule before the handover is an accident of this branch only (D2069).
- **CI does not run the alert rule tests of this feature or of ADR-0081**: the step `promtool test rules` of `.github/workflows/ci.yml` runs only `batch_stalled_test.yaml`. `trigger_schedules_test.yaml` and `instance_registry_test.yaml` exist and pass locally (`promtool test rules`, the image of the CI step); the line to add after `/tests/batch_stalled_test.yaml` is ` /tests/instance_registry_test.yaml /tests/trigger_schedules_test.yaml` (G2022). The workflow is not touched by this change because a change of it reruns the whole stack.
