# ADR-0091: A one-shot job, with a role that can do only that, hands the cron triggers over after a deployment, once the fleet is stable and converged

**Status:** Proposed (2026-11-14; revised four times the same day, after the first to fourth reviews)
**Decided by:** owner for the behaviour and the mechanism (OD-2026-11-14-4 "Automático no upgrade"; OD-2026-11-14-5 "Job pós-deploy"; OD-2026-11-14-6, the opt-out; OD-2026-11-14-7, the tenant audit; OD-2026-11-14-12 "Aceitar as duas", the plain Job and the role `sagat_handover`, which answered O1957 and refines OD-2026-11-14-5); agent for the details (tasks T018-082 to T018-087). The first draft (a definer function the runtime role calls) was replaced after the architecture, security and correctness reviews; the second (`5ba3c52e`) had no Blocker; the third (`4c0a5d96`) found the text not ready to accept; the fourth (`8c8caf58`) found one Major and some Minors, answered here. Not accepted.
**Refines / supersedes:** amends ADR-0083 decision 4 (the handover is made by the operator's command, run by the operator or by a job the deployment runs on the operator's behalf) and its accepted limit on audit (D2042); extends ADR-0083 decision 5 (what the command does outside its transaction). **Amends ADR-0082 decision 1 in one respect:** the operator command may also run with the handover role `sagat_handover`, which can record `schedules-v1` for the engines through one wrapper and nothing else; "never the runtime role's" stands whole. Amends `plan.md` of feature 018 ("the registry ships in a release before the handover can be used").
**Enforced by:** nothing yet; the checks are tasks (T018-085). Until they exist and run this ADR is a design (constitution, rule 11).

## Context

Feature 018 hands the firing of the cron triggers from the legacy cron pass to the schedule pass once and one way (ADR-0083). Today an operator runs `control-plane schedule-trigger-handover --apply` with the schema-owning role: in one bounded transaction it records `schedules-v1` for every live engine through the registry and moves the state (`apps/control-plane/src/handover.rs:344-449`; `crates/infra-postgres/src/instances.rs:56-63`, `:161-209`). The owner decided that it happens during the upgrade without an operator (OD-2026-11-14-4), then, after the first review, how: a one-shot job that runs the same command and waits, bounded, until its conditions hold (OD-2026-11-14-5).

What the reviews established, and what constrains the job:

1. **The runtime role writes most of what a census reads.** `sagat_app` holds `INSERT, UPDATE, DELETE` on `scheduler_members` (`crates/infra-postgres/migrations/20261017000000_scheduler_shards.up.sql:92`), and `instance_register` takes the kind, the scheduler owner and the capabilities from its caller (`20270113000000_instance_registry.up.sql:180-183`, `:228-236`). It also holds `INSERT` on `audit_records` (`20261008000200_runtime_role_grants.up.sql:20`), whose primary key is `id` (`20261008000000_identity.up.sql:56`).
2. **An engine that has just left is invisible.** The census counts rows seen in the last 30 s (`20270114000000_trigger_handover.up.sql:217-218`); a clean stop deletes the registry row (`20270113000000_instance_registry.up.sql:280-287`) and the scheduler membership (`crates/infra-postgres/src/partitioning.rs:90-91`); expired memberships are deleted after an hour (`partitioning.rs:75`). The command answers departures today with `--expect-live N` (`handover.rs:163-189`), which a deployment with an autoscaler cannot give (`infra/helm/values.yaml:165-180`).
3. **The registry cannot see a 0.2 binary**, and it ships in the release that hands over, against the premise of its own migration (`20270113000000_instance_registry.up.sql:20-21`). A 0.2 engine still announces itself in `scheduler_members` (`v0.2.0:crates/infra-postgres/src/partitioning.rs`, lines 51-60); a 0.2 control plane leaves no row anywhere, but a trigger it publishes has no schedule until the reconciliation makes one (G2020).
4. **`migrate` runs before the engines and must finish first** (`infra/docker/compose.yaml:162-164`, `:205-207`). The Helm chart runs no migration and holds no owner credential (`infra/helm/templates/NOTES.txt:11`).
5. **Helm waits for a hook, and a hook is not part of the release.** A `post-upgrade` hook makes `helm upgrade` wait for it (default `--timeout` 5 min), and a release rolled back by `--atomic` or `helm rollback` does not delete it, so a hook Job that waits a horizon (10 min) fails the upgrade, the images go back to 0.2, and the Job goes on looping for its wait.
6. **The command maps only its own words to "try again"**, by matching text: a lock timeout on the epoch (`55P03`) or a statement timeout (`57014`) is a database error with exit code 1 (`handover.rs:481-494`; `apps/control-plane/src/lib.rs:96-110`), and refusals that a wait cures (`handover.rs:305`, `:478`, `:483`) are `Declined`, exit code 2. The convergence count gives itself 4 s (`crates/infra-postgres/src/trigger_schedules.rs:786`), more than the activation's 3 s statement timeout.
7. **The audit.** The handover row records the operator and the database role and no mode (`20270114000000_trigger_handover.up.sql:186-189`); `audit_records.tenant_id` is `NOT NULL` (`identity.up.sql:55-66`).

## Decision

1. **Who acts: a one-shot Job of the release, with the handover role.** It runs `control-plane schedule-trigger-handover --auto`, which drives the rounds and the activation of decision 2 through the functions of decision 9; **the database decides**, so a holder of the role's password who calls those functions from `psql` gets no weaker handover than the job.
   - **Helm:** a plain Job (not a hook; OD-2026-11-14-12) whose name is `<release>-schedule-trigger-handover-<revision>` cut to 52 characters, then `-` and the first 10 hex digits of the SHA-256 of the untruncated name, so it never exceeds 63 (the `job-name` label's limit); `restartPolicy: Never`; `ttlSecondsAfterFinished` (1 h); `backoffLimit` 2; `activeDeadlineSeconds` the wait plus five minutes; a `podFailurePolicy` that fails it at once on exit code 2 and ignores a pod with the condition `DisruptionTarget` (a drain or a preemption does not use up the retries); a service account of its own with `automountServiceAccountToken: false`; the role's `DATABASE_URL` from the Secret `schedules.autoHandover.databaseSecret`, which the chart `required`s when `schedules.autoHandover` is true (when false the chart renders neither the Job nor any reference to the Secret); and `SAGAT_HANDOVER_RUN_ID` from the pod's label `batch.kubernetes.io/controller-uid` through the downward API (the UID of the Job object: the same for a retried pod, new for every Job, including the one `helm rollback` recreates under an old revision's name). Helm does not wait for a plain Job (without `--wait-for-jobs`), and the next upgrade or a rollback deletes the Job of the revision it leaves; Helm 3 deletes with background propagation, so the garbage collector then deletes the pod, which gets SIGTERM and its grace period (Helm's documented behaviour, not observed here: G2250).
   - **Compose:** a service with `restart: "no"`, after the control plane and the engines have started, with the role's URL and a run id drawn at the start of the process (compose does not retry it).
   - **SIGTERM:** the command MUST NOT start an activation after it has received SIGTERM or SIGINT; it ends the round it is in, writes `INTERRUPTED` to the attempt row and exits 4. An activation already begun is bounded (3 s, decision 4) and either commits or rolls back within the pod's grace period (30 s).
   - **The handover role (D2264).** `sagat_handover` (`NOSUPERUSER NOCREATEDB NOCREATEROLE NOREPLICATION NOBYPASSRLS`, owning nothing, a member of no role) is ensured by **every `migrate` run**, after the migrations: created `NOLOGIN` if it is missing and the migrating role may create roles (otherwise `migrate` says the operator must create it, and goes on), then granted again by an owner function that holds every grant of the role (so a role created after the migration that introduced it still gets them) and that **refuses** when an existing `sagat_handover` is wider than that (an attribute beyond `LOGIN`, or a membership in any role: D2275). Given `SAGAT_HANDOVER_PASSWORD`, `migrate` sets `LOGIN` and the password **only while the state is `LEGACY` and the retirement row is empty** (D2269), and sends a SCRAM-SHA-256 verifier it computed itself, never the clear password, so neither `log_statement` nor an error context can log it (D2272, G2254). On PostgreSQL 16 or later, a role the operator created can be altered only by a role with `ADMIN OPTION` on it; `migrate` then gets `42501`, warns with the remedy (`GRANT sagat_handover TO <migrating role> WITH ADMIN OPTION`, or the statement run by the role's creator) and goes on (D2274). The job takes the password apart from the URL (`SAGAT_HANDOVER_PASSWORD` given to the connection options), so a password with `@`, `/` or `#` cannot break an interpolated URL (D2273). `postgres-init` does not create it, so a fresh volume initialises without the password and an operator who opts out never needs one. Compose passes `SAGAT_HANDOVER_PASSWORD` to `migrate` and to the job; with the job on and no password the job exits 2 at once, saying which variable to set. In Helm the operator runs `migrate` (as today) with the variable, or creates the login once. The operator's `--apply` runs only as the owner: `sagat_handover` can make only the automatic handover (decision 9).
   - **Retiring the role (D2265).** Deleting a Secret revokes nothing, and compose has none. After `HANDED_OVER` and the completion of the audit, `--retire-role` (Helm `schedules.autoHandover.retireRole`, compose `SAGAT_HANDOVER_RETIRE_ROLE`, both off by default: the operator allows it) calls an owner function that runs `ALTER ROLE sagat_handover NOLOGIN` **and records the retirement** (who, when) in an owner-only singleton that `migrate` reads, so a later `migrate` with the password still in `.env` does not log the role in again (D2269); where the alteration is refused (`42501`, D2274) the job warns with the remedy and the runbook gives the statement. With `--retire-role`, a later job that cannot log in (`28000`) exits 0 saying the role may be retired; `28000` is also a `pg_hba` refusal, which is why the engines alert on a missing or stale attempt row (decision 5). The runtime role MUST get no new privilege.

2. **The condition: the command's census, made stable, with two detectors.** `--auto` MUST hand over only when, in `--stable N` consecutive rounds (default 3) spaced at least one heartbeat apart (15 s), and no sooner than one horizon after the job's first round (so that a horizon of evidence exists even with no backlog), all of these hold, and then again under the locks of the activation:
   - every engine seen within `--horizon` (default 10 min, between 10 min and 1 h: the floor is the database's, and expired memberships are deleted after an hour) lists `schedules-v1`;
   - the set of live engine instances is the same in every round and has at least `--min-live M` members (default 1; Helm passes the engine's `replicas` or the autoscaler's minimum);
   - no `scheduler_members` row whose `expires_at` is within the horizon (expired ones count as present) has an owner that no engine row seen within the horizon names as its `scheduler_owner` (a 0.2 engine);
   - no trigger-owned schedule was made, revived, respecified, resumed or removed by the reconciliation within the horizon (`schedules.reconciled_at`; only a control plane older than this release leaves that work to it, and the backlog of an upgrade leaves the same mark);
   - at least one control plane is live in the registry;
   - the drift view is empty.

   The rounds are made by the database: each call of the round function (decision 9) computes every condition itself, keeps the run's first round time, horizon and deadline when it first sees the run id, and counts the consecutive rounds, at least 15 s apart, in which every condition held and the live set was the same. The activation wrapper takes the epoch lock, **computes every condition again under it** (the capability over the horizon, at least one live engine and the run's `--min-live`, no unregistered membership, no reconciliation mark within the horizon, a live control plane, the first horizon passed, the run's stable count reached with the same live set) and only then calls the owner-only activation and completion, so the Rust checks (`handover.rs:297-325`) are a second line, not the first. The wrapper MUST refuse unless `session_user` is exactly `sagat_handover` (a member of the role who logs in as itself is refused) and unless the run's last stable round is younger than one heartbeat (12 s) on the database's clock (D2271). The mode is `AUTOMATIC` because of that `session_user`, and the actor is `system:handover-job:<run id>`, where the run id is a `uuid` parameter, never text, since it reaches every tenant's audit; neither the mode nor the actor is an argument. `--expect-live N` stays for the operator's `--apply`.

3. **No transaction is open across a wait.** Each round is short reads; the waits hold no connection; the activation is the existing bounded transaction (ADR-0082 decision 2), attempted only after a stable round. `--wait` (default 2 h, at most 24 h) bounds the job.

4. **Timeouts and refusals are typed; what a wait cures is retried.** The command MUST return a typed reason, never match text: `55P03`, `57014`, `HANDOVER_BUSY`, the activation's step bound, a change of the live count under the lock (`handover.rs:305`) and a state conflict (`:478`) are reasons that a wait cures, retried by `--auto` with a backoff (15 s doubling to 5 min); three `57014` in a row are reported as `CHECK_TOO_SLOW`, not `BUSY`. The rounds run with a statement timeout of 5 s (outside the epoch; the convergence count needs 4 s), the activation with 3 s, which exceeds its longest single lock wait (2 s) plus its work; its step bound (3 s) exceeds the waits while the epoch is held (the table, 2 s; the handover row does not wait, since handovers serialise on the epoch) plus the work; both stay under the 5 s statement timeout of the processes that wait for the epoch.

5. **A blocked handover says why.** The closed reasons are `ENGINE_LACKS_CAPABILITY`, `ENGINE_UNREGISTERED`, `TOO_FEW_ENGINES`, `FLEET_CHANGING`, `RECONCILIATION_RECENT`, `NO_CONTROL_PLANE`, `UNCONVERGED`, `FIRST_HORIZON`, `BUSY`, `CHECK_TOO_SLOW`, `INTERRUPTED` and `DEADLINE`. Each round writes the last reason, its count and the time to the attempt row; every engine reads it with the handover state and exports `trigger_schedule_handover_blocked{reason}` (series at zero; 1 for the last reason while `LEGACY`). `SagatTriggerHandoverBlocked` MUST be `max without (reason) (trigger_schedule_handover_blocked) == 1` for one hour. Every engine also exports `trigger_schedule_handover_mode{mode}` (1 for the `changed_mode` it reads, 0 for the other) — the one-shot job exports nothing, because it is not scraped. A test MUST hold the reason codes of the SQL, the Rust enum, the runbook anchors and the rows of runbook 6.3 to one set. Exit codes: **0** handed over, already handed over, or switched off; **2** a reason no wait cures (a credential without the grants, a state this build does not know); **3** the deadline, with the last reason; **4** interrupted; **1** a database failure. One test per call site of a refusal holds its code. **A job that never writes is seen too (D2270):** every engine exports `trigger_schedule_handover_attempt_age_seconds` (since the attempt row's last round; the series is absent while no row exists) and `trigger_schedule_auto_handover_expected` (1 when the deployment runs the job: the chart and compose pass `schedules.autoHandover` to the engines for this gauge only, and no engine decides anything on it), and `SagatTriggerHandoverNotAttempted` fires while the state is `LEGACY`, the job is expected, and the row is missing or older than 15 minutes, for 30 minutes: a `pg_hba` refusal, a missing role or a missing password.

6. **The opt-out is a deployment value (OD-2026-11-14-6).** `schedules.autoHandover: false` leaves the Job out of the chart; `SAGAT_SCHEDULES_AUTO_HANDOVER=false` makes the compose service exit 0 at once. Both default to on. The engines receive it only to export `trigger_schedule_auto_handover_expected` (decision 5); nothing they do depends on it.

7. **Audit (OD-2026-11-14-7).** The handover row MUST gain `changed_mode` (`OPERATOR` | `AUTOMATIC`; the migration backfills `OPERATOR` on a row already handed over) and `changed_epoch`. In the activation's transaction, owner-only and keyed by the epoch: the census (instance id, kind, binary version, `started_at`, `last_seen` of every serving instance seen within the horizon; the registry holds at most 1,000 rows, so it is copied whole and a census that does not fit refuses, never truncates), and **the tenant set**: one row per tenant that has a cron trigger, each with a random `audit_id` drawn there and `audited_at` null. **After** the commit the completion function writes, for every row of that set not yet audited, the `audit_records` row with id `audit_id` (action `schedule.trigger_handover`, resource `platform`/`schedule_trigger_handover`, actor, mode and time **from the handover row**) and sets `audited_at`; an id already present (only a row the runtime role could not have guessed) is compared and, if it differs, refused (`AUDIT_CONFLICT`). A run that finds the state handed over completes the set before it exits 0, so a job that died between the commit and the rows is completed by its retry or the next run, and only for tenants the handover affected. One log line gives the mode, the epoch, the engines and the **count** of tenants.

9. **Rules for every new function (normative).** Every function this ADR adds MUST be `SECURITY DEFINER` only when it must be, MUST carry `SET search_path = pg_catalog, pg_temp`, MUST be `REVOKE ALL ... FROM PUBLIC` and granted by name, and MUST take no argument that weakens a condition: the round function takes the run id (`uuid`) and the job's own stricter values (`--stable`, `--horizon`, `--min-live`, each floored in SQL), the wrapper takes the run id only. In compose these functions are owned by the superuser that runs `migrate` (`infra/docker/compose.yaml:15`), so the typed parameters and the pinned `search_path` are what keep them narrow. A test reads the catalog and fails on any function of the schema that is `SECURITY DEFINER` without the pinned `search_path` or is executable by `PUBLIC`. The functions `sagat_handover` may execute are the round function, the wrapper, the audit completion and the retirement, and `SELECT` on the handover and attempt rows; the dry run checks the wrapper's privilege, not `platform_requirement_activate` (`handover.rs:379-386`), and `--auto` reads its census through the round function, not the owner-only census (`handover.rs:364`).

10. **One-way, unchanged.** Nothing here adds a way back (`20270114000000_trigger_handover.up.sql:105-121`, `:225-238`). The new migration's `down` MUST refuse while any handover row has a mode.

## Considered options

1. **(a) A `SECURITY DEFINER` function the engines call** (the first draft). Lost: `sagat_app` writes the scheduler memberships and chooses what it registers (Context 1), so the function would decide on facts its caller can forge, and it narrowed ADR-0082 decision 1 for the runtime role.
2. **An engine-only database role that alone may execute that function.** Lost: the engines still write the memberships and the registry rows; and splitting the runtime role touches every grant of every feature.
3. **(b) `migrate`, waiting.** Lost: the engines start after `migrate` has finished (Context 4).
4. **(c) The control plane at start-up.** Lost: it runs as the runtime role, so it needs (a).
5. **(d) A long-running process with an owner or handover credential.** Lost: the credential would live as long as the platform.
6. **(e) A one-shot Job running the tested command (chosen, OD-2026-11-14-5, refined by OD-2026-11-14-12)**, with the handover role and as a plain Job of the release. The runtime role gains nothing; the credential is in the cluster from the upgrade until the operator deletes the Secret, and it can record one requirement and nothing else (not the owner's, which would have been in the cluster at every upgrade and can do anything); a rolled-back release deletes its Job. OD-2026-11-14-12 accepted it in place of the letter of OD-2026-11-14-5 (a `post-upgrade` hook with the owner's credentials).
7. **(e′) The same job as a `post-upgrade` hook.** Lost: Context 5.
8. **(f) Defer the automatic handover to 0.4.0.** Not chosen by the owner.

## Consequences

Good: a default upgrade hands over by itself at least a horizon after the job began, once the fleet holds still, the backlog is made and no sign of an old process is seen; the runtime role gains nothing; the chart holds a credential that can make only the automatic handover, under conditions the database checks, and only until the role is retired; a rollback stops the job; tenants see the handover in their audit, and only those it affected.

Bad and accepted:
- a 0.2 engine started after the handover still ignores a pause (G2240); an old control plane that publishes nothing for a horizon and then publishes loses that trigger's first ticks (G2241);
- the job decides on rows the runtime role can write, so a holder of `sagat_app` can make the handover come **early** (delete or shadow a 0.2 membership, clear a reconciliation mark, register a control-plane row) as well as delay it; that is no new power, since the same role can start any run, and it cannot record the requirement (G2243);
- an engine that stopped cleanly leaves no row, so `--min-live` is the only guard against a fleet that is partly down (G2245);
- a fleet that never holds still is never handed over by the job (G2242);
- the tenant set is recorded under the table lock, at a cost not yet measured (G2248); OD-2026-11-14-7 kept the audit rows out of the locks, and they are;
- one `helm upgrade` rolls engines and control planes together, so OD-2026-11-14-3's order is not what a single release does (G2246);
- `migrate` may not be allowed to create or alter roles on a managed database, and the operator then does it by hand (G2249); a job that cannot log in writes no attempt row, which the second alert of decision 5 catches (G2251, mostly closed); the statement that sets the password carries its SCRAM verifier, which an operator's logs may keep (G2254); `sagat_app` can still insert `schedule.trigger_handover` audit rows with ids of its own, a power it had before this ADR (G2252); compose has no health checks (G2247); SIGTERM and Helm's deletion are tested at the process and by `helm template`, not in a cluster (G2250).

Operational cost: a few reads every 15 s for at most `--wait`; one bounded activation; one insert per affected tenant after it.

Compatibility with runs in flight: none changes; the gate of the legacy pass, the shared key, the control window and the reconciliation are ADR-0083's.

**What the release notes of 0.3.0 MUST say** (D2248): the handover is automatic after a deployment, its conditions, and how to see that it happened (`trigger_schedule_handover_mode`, the log line, `SELECT state, changed_mode, changed_at FROM schedule_trigger_handover`, the tenant audit); it is one-way (no command back, the schema `down` refuses, no engine below 0.3.0 after it); give `migrate` `SAGAT_HANDOVER_PASSWORD` (or create the login once), put the role's URL in the Secret, and after the handover retire the role (`--retire-role`, or `ALTER ROLE sagat_handover NOLOGIN`) and delete the Secret; `schedules.autoHandover: false` or `SAGAT_SCHEDULES_AUTO_HANDOVER=false` keeps it manual; the accepted losses G2005, G2020 and G2021; an engine with `SAGAT_SCHEDULES_ENABLED=false` holds it.

**Rollback.** Before the handover: the opt-out, or a release rolled back (which deletes the Job) and the schema `down`. After it: not supported, as today.

## Test plan (T018-085; every test written first and seen failing for the right reason)

Races are held by an open transaction or a held lock and watched in `pg_stat_activity` (`wait_event_type = 'Lock'`), never timed.

- **An engine registering without `schedules-v1`, both orders**; **a publication in flight, both orders**; **the job and the operator at once** (one epoch, one history row, one census copy, one tenant set, one audit row per tenant).
- **The departure case**: an engine stops between two rounds (`FLEET_CHANGING`), between the last round and the lock (refused under the lock), crashed without `schedules-v1` within the horizon; fewer than `--min-live`.
- **The detectors and the first horizon**: a live and a recently expired 0.2 membership; a trigger inserted without its schedule, then made, revived, respecified (`trigger_schedules.rs:877-946`) or resumed (`:1020`) by the reconciliation, each setting `reconciled_at`; a fleet with no backlog waits `FIRST_HORIZON`; times backdated as the owner, never slept.
- **The forgeries of G2243**, one test each, written to show what the job then does: a 0.2 membership deleted, a membership shadowed by a registry row naming it, a mark cleared, a control-plane row registered by `sagat_app`.
- **The audit**: `sagat_app` pre-inserts rows with every id it can derive and with a guessed one: the completion writes its own rows (the ids are not guessable) and refuses `AUDIT_CONFLICT` on a planted id that differs; a crash after the commit is completed once, for the recorded set only, with the handover row's actor, mode and time, also when a tenant gained or lost its last cron trigger in between.
- **Timeouts and refusals**: `57014`, `55P03`, the step bound, `handover.rs:305` and `:478` are retried with the backoff; three `57014` are `CHECK_TOO_SLOW`; one test per refusal call site holds its exit code.
- **SIGTERM**: the command in `--auto`, held between a stable round and its activation by a barrier, receives SIGTERM: it exits 4, writes `INTERRUPTED`, and the state is `LEGACY`; received during an activation, the activation commits or rolls back whole.
- **The job's resume**: a retried pod with the same run id keeps the first round's time and the deadline; another run id does not.
- **The roles**: nothing new is executable by `sagat_app`; the auto-handover suites run **as `sagat_handover`** (as `SAGAT_TEST_AS_RUNTIME_ROLE` does for `sagat_app`); a privilege audit of `sagat_handover` with its own `ALLOWED` list, built on `runtime_privileges.rs`, over the statements of the command; `sagat_handover` can call only its four functions (no table write, no other capability, no removal, not the owner's census or `platform_requirement_activate`); the catalog test of decision 9.
- **The wrapper alone** (`psql` as `sagat_handover`, no job): with each condition false in turn (an unregistered membership, a recent mark, no control plane, fewer than `--min-live` live, the first horizon not passed, too few stable rounds, a live set that changed) the wrapper refuses with that reason and records nothing; the mode is `AUTOMATIC` and the actor `system:handover-job` whatever the caller says.
- **The role on an upgrade**: a database migrated from 0.2 without the role gets it `NOLOGIN` from `migrate`, with every grant; created by hand after the migration, it gets them at the next `migrate`; `SAGAT_HANDOVER_PASSWORD` makes it log in; without it a fresh compose volume initialises and the job exits 2; `--retire-role` leaves it `NOLOGIN` and a later job exits 0.
- **The fourth review**: `migrate` with the password after a retirement leaves the role `NOLOGIN`, and after `HANDED_OVER` without a retirement sets nothing; `log_statement = all` while `migrate` sets the password shows no clear password; a password with `@`, `/` and `#` connects; a role created by another `CREATEROLE` role on PostgreSQL 16 makes `migrate` warn and go on; a wider role (a membership, `BYPASSRLS`) is refused by the grant function; a member of `sagat_handover` logging in as itself and a stale last round are refused by the wrapper; `SagatTriggerHandoverNotAttempted` fires with no row and with a stale one, not when the job is not expected, in a `promtool` test.
- **The run id**: a Job recreated by `helm rollback` under an old revision's name has a new controller UID and starts a new run (a new first round and deadline).
- **The migration**: additive; the backfill of `OPERATOR`; `down` refuses for any mode; up, down, up with data.
- **The deployment**: `helm template` renders a plain Job whose name is at most 63 characters for a 53-character release name and a five-digit revision, with `restartPolicy: Never`, its TTL, limits, the `DisruptionTarget: Ignore` rule, the run id from the controller UID, a service account without a token and the required Secret, fails without the Secret when on, and renders nothing when off (`contract_scalability_ha`); a compose smoke test (exit 0, and exit 0 doing nothing when off).
