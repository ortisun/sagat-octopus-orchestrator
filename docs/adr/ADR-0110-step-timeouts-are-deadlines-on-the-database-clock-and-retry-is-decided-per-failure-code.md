# ADR-0110: The timeouts of a leased step are deadlines on the database clock, only a deadline that ends a live attempt leaves its outcome unknown, and retry is decided per failure code

**Status:** Proposed
**Decided by:** agent, proposing for design 045 under the owner's delegation (Owner decision 2026-11-20-6, widened by Owner decision 2026-11-24-4)
**Refines / supersedes:** refines the retry rules of feature 002 (`RetryPolicy`, Decision 26 of `specs/002-durable-execution/research.md`) and the lease rules of `docs/architecture/execution-semantics.md`; refines [ADR-0089](ADR-0089-a-gated-step-waits-outside-ready-and-one-fenced-grant-routine-serves-every-waiter-by-key.md) decisions 4 and 5 (a waiter's deadline and eligibility gain the close cutoff), [ADR-0082](ADR-0082-the-instance-registry-records-requirements-only-for-the-operator-and-a-refused-process-stays-up-with-a-closed-gate.md) decision 1 (the runtime role holds the requirement's row `FOR SHARE` for a publication, as Architecture decision 0098's build does), [ADR-0029](ADR-0029-worker-task-streams-spend-finite-receive-credit.md) decision 6 (a handler-sourced lease is not renewed continuously by the host) and Architecture decision 0099 decision 5 (the next lease of a withdrawn attempt keeps, or freshly extends, the start deadline); keeps feature 037 and Architecture decision 0076 unchanged; composes with Architecture decisions 0098 and 0099 (lease withdrawal and delivery acknowledgement, not merged). Supersedes none.
**Enforced by:** to be built with design 045 (`docs/designs/045-activity-timeouts.md`, Detailed Implementation Plan): the unit tests of the retry decision table (root, scoped and path steps) and of the deadline arithmetic in `crates/domain`; the replay-equals-projection test comparing the deadline columns; the barrier tests of a claim, a completion, a withdrawal, a gate, a pause, a reconciliation and an operator's retry against each deadline (`tests/fault/activity-timeouts/`, `fault_activity_timeouts`); the parity test of claim eligibility between the candidate query and the domain's lease decision; a rule in `scripts/check-architecture.py` that the deadline columns are assigned only from event values; the parity test of the outcome-unknown codes (`crates/domain/tests/outcome_unknown_parity.rs`), which must pass unchanged; the claim budget lines of `integration_claim_transition_efficiency`, which must not grow; the registry tests of the two requirements and their replay floor; the conformance scenarios `activity-timeouts`, `retry-by-code` and `heartbeat-from-handler` in six languages.

## Context

A Temporal activity has four timeouts: schedule-to-start (a task waits in its queue), start-to-close (one attempt runs), schedule-to-close (all attempts and their waits) and heartbeat (an attempt stops making progress), and a retry policy whose `nonRetryableErrorTypes` makes some errors final. This platform has two of them. A node's `timeoutMs` is an attempt deadline fixed at the claim (`steps.attempt_deadline_at`, which no renewal extends), and the lease expiry (`steps.lease_expires_at`, 30 seconds by default for every node, `lease_ttl` in `crates/application/src/worker.rs`) is a heartbeat timeout that is the same for every activity. The retry policy (`crates/domain/src/workflow.rs`) has no rule per error: a worker's `retryable` flag is the only way to make a failure final.

Three facts of this platform shape the design and differ from Temporal:

- **An attempt that ends without its report has an unknown outcome** (feature 037, constitution principle III). `LEASE_EXPIRED` and `ATTEMPT_TIMEOUT` send a declared activity to reconciliation or to a person, never to an automatic retry (`end_attempt` in `crates/domain/src/execution/command.rs`). Temporal retries a timed-out activity because it assumes activities are idempotent; this platform does not assume it.
- **The worker already names its failure.** `FailStep.error_code` is a machine code that every SDK restricts to `[A-Z0-9_]{1,64}` (`ActivityFailure(code, message, retryable)`, `sdks/SPEC.md` §6.5). It is the error type.
- **Time is the database's.** A run transaction reads the database's time under the run lock (`db_now`); scans compare with the database's `now()`; a pass re-checks under the run lock before it acts (`lost_leases` in `crates/infra-postgres/src/maintenance.rs`, the passes of `crates/scheduler/src/passes.rs`).

## Decision

1. **Four timeouts, one meaning each, all optional, on `activity` and `script` nodes of the forward path, in the root graph and in bodies.** `timeoutMs` keeps its meaning: start-to-close of one attempt. `scheduleToStartMs` bounds the time from the moment an attempt becomes claimable (`READY`) to its lease. `scheduleToCloseMs` bounds the time from the step's first release to its end, every attempt, retry wait and gate wait included. `heartbeatTimeoutMs` (18,000 to 86,400,000 ms, so that every lease can be provisional under protocol 1.8) replaces the platform's lease time-to-live for the leases of that node. Compensation and reconciliation nodes keep today's rules, and the close deadline does not bound a reconciliation: a deadline MUST NOT end a pending compensation, a reconciliation or a review (constitution principle III).
2. **Each deadline is computed by the domain from `db_now` when the transition that starts it happens, recorded in that transition's event, and written to its column from the event.** The start deadline is set when the attempt becomes claimable and kept through its lease, removed while the run is paused, and extended by a withdrawal to a full window from the withdrawal (the withdrawn lease never started). The close deadline and its cutoff (the close deadline less the close floor, the shorter of `timeoutMs` and 6,000 ms) are set at the step's first release, kept by every later attempt, and cleared when a reconciliation or a review takes over or an operator retries a known failure. The fields are read, and the events carry them, only for a version whose level (recorded at publication) is above 0. An attempt's deadline at its claim MUST be the earlier of the claim's time plus `timeoutMs` and the close deadline, and the step records which one it is.
3. **The lease decision is the domain's and never leases a step past its deadline.** It refuses a step whose start deadline or close cutoff has passed, or that needs host features the session lacks (the handler source, or a `heartbeatTimeoutMs` below 30,000 ms, which only a host that reads the time-to-live as a duration is given; such a node must declare `scheduleToStartMs`); the candidate query carries the same conditions as a hint, so unclaimable rows do not fill a poll's window. A pass of its own then ends the step under the run lock, re-checking the shard fence and the step. A pause removes the claimable steps' start deadlines and the resume gives each a full window; the close deadline is not moved and is enforced during a pause. A withdrawal extends the start deadline to a full window from the withdrawal and keeps the step's place in the queue, so that a delivery lost on the wire does not fail a step whose workers are up; no claimable transition writes a start deadline while the run is paused.
4. **A deadline that ends no live attempt is a known failure; a deadline that ends a live attempt is an unknown outcome.** `SCHEDULE_TO_START_TIMEOUT` and `SCHEDULE_TO_CLOSE_TIMEOUT` reached while no attempt is live end the step `FAILED` with that code, are not retried, and take the node's failure route; a worker cannot report either code (it is recorded `WORKER_ERROR`). A close deadline reached during an attempt ends that attempt as its attempt deadline: `ATTEMPT_TIMEOUT` with `deadline: scheduleToClose`, and the step goes to reconciliation or review as feature 037 says. A silent attempt ends `LEASE_EXPIRED` whatever its time-to-live, with `deadline: heartbeat` when the node declares `heartbeatTimeoutMs`; no outcome-unknown code is added.
5. **Retry is decided per failure code, by one function for root, scoped and path steps, after the rules that already come first.** The order MUST be: an outcome-unknown code goes to reconciliation or review; a step asked to stop is cancelled; a worker's `retryable = false` is final; a code in `retry.nonRetryableErrorCodes` is final; the effective policy is the base policy overlaid by the one entry of `retry.byErrorCode` that names the code, and is spent when the step's attempt count reaches its `maxAttempts`; another attempt is scheduled only when its time is before the close cutoff (at or after it is final), and otherwise the step fails `SCHEDULE_TO_CLOSE_TIMEOUT` with the last attempt's code recorded. A code that the platform decides elsewhere MUST NOT appear in either list, and each effective policy MUST be valid; publication refuses both.
6. **A worker may ask for a longer delay before the next attempt.** `FailStep.next_retry_delay_ms` (protocol 1.9) replaces the computed delay, without jitter, for that one retry, the longer of it and the policy's computed delay, at most 24 hours; the event records that the delay came from the worker; it does not add an attempt and it cannot pass the close cutoff.
7. **A handler can be the source of the heartbeat.** With `heartbeatFrom: "handler"` (which requires `heartbeatTimeoutMs` and `scheduleToStartMs`, so that such a step cannot wait without end for a host that can run it) a host renews the lease only after its handler called `heartbeat()` since the last renewal, so a handler that stops making progress loses its lease, which is Temporal's meaning of a heartbeat. Such a node is leased only to a session at protocol 1.9 or later. With `"host"`, the default, the host renews while the handler runs, as today.
8. **The rules are switched on by the fleet, with a replay floor.** A version that uses any field of this decision publishes only once the operator recorded `activity-timeouts-v1`, and one that needs protocol 1.9 hosts also `activity-timeouts-protocol-v1`, which only a build that offers that minor lists; both are recorded for control planes and engines. Publication holds each requirement until it commits, through a definer function that reads the requirement's own row `FOR SHARE` and checks that it is recorded for both process kinds, as Architecture decision 0098's `lease_withdrawal_required()` does, inside the store's publication transaction. A rule of Architecture decision 0098's replay-floor trigger MUST refuse removing a requirement while any workflow version of the level that needs it exists, retired included, because an older build can neither replay its runs nor honour it: once used, this release is the floor for a rollback. A version published before keeps today's behaviour for every run in flight.

## Considered options

1. **Deadlines computed by the domain, journaled, kept on step rows; a lease decision and a candidate query that respect them; retry per code on `error_code` (chosen).** No new statement on the claim or the completion, one pass with its own window, the outcome-unknown rule untouched, and every decision journaled.
2. **Temporal's semantics as they are: retry after every timeout.** Simpler to explain to a Temporal user, but it repeats effects whose outcome is unknown, which constitution principle III and feature 037 forbid. Rejected.
3. **Timers in the `timers` machinery (one timer row per deadline).** One more row and one more statement per attempt for every node that declares a timeout, on the hot path that design 038 measured at 38 statements per lease. Rejected for the cost; the step row already carries the attempt deadline.
4. **A new `HEARTBEAT_TIMEOUT` code.** It would mean what `LEASE_EXPIRED` means, grow the outcome-unknown list and its copies, and give every consumer two codes to match. Rejected after the zoom-out review of design 045; the `deadline` field says which bound passed.
5. **A new error-type field on `FailStep`.** Two names for one fact (`error_code` already is the type), a fork from what six SDKs send today. Rejected; the SDKs instead map exception classes to codes locally.
6. **Patterns or prefixes in `nonRetryableErrorCodes`.** Convenient, but a policy read by a person must say exactly which codes it names; exact codes keep the decision auditable. Left out of 0.3.0; addable later without breaking a version.
7. **A minimum heartbeat timeout of 10 s, a pause that shifts deadlines, and a replay floor that scans journals.** Rejected in the second review round: a lease under 18 s loses delivery acknowledgement's protection; a shift is wrong for steps that became claimable during the pause and needs a payload per step; a journal scan cannot tell this design's transitions from existing ones and does not fit the operator's statement timeout.
8. **The conditions only in the run transaction, or deadlines computed in SQL.** Rejected after the architecture and correctness reviews: the first lets unclaimable rows fill an older host's candidate window; the second gives a column two derivations, so the projection could differ from the replay.

## Consequences

- A step that cannot find a worker, or whose retries outlive their budget, ends by a rule the author chose instead of waiting forever or until its run deadline.
- A user who comes from Temporal sees a timed-out attempt wait for reconciliation or review instead of retrying. The design doc says so, names `reconcileWith` as the way to make it automatic, and the Studio shows the reason. A `script` node, which cannot declare `reconcileWith`, goes to review.
- Operational cost: six columns and two partial indexes on `steps` and one column on `workflow_versions`, built by reading the table once at the upgrade; one pass more per shard; a lease of a node with the minimum heartbeat timeout of 18 s beats five thirds as often as today (every 6 s against every 10 s); a node with a long heartbeat timeout holds a dead worker's step and its concurrency slot for that long; once a version uses the fields, rollback below this release is refused.
- The outcome-unknown list and its copies do not change. Two known-failure codes are added.
- An attempt leased less than 18 s before its close deadline is not provisional under protocol 1.8, because its attempt deadline is its earlier deadline; a delivery lost on the wire there ends as an unknown outcome (Gap 045-005 of the design's register).
- Rollback: before a requirement is recorded, nothing uses the fields; after, it can be removed only while no version of its level exists.
