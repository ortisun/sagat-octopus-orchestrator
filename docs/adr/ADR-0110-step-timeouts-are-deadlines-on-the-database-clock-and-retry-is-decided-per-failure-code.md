# ADR-0110: The timeouts of a leased step are deadlines on the database clock, only a deadline that ends a live attempt leaves its outcome unknown, and retry is decided per failure code

**Status:** Proposed
**Decided by:** agent, proposing for design 045 under the owner's delegation (Owner decision 2026-11-20-6, widened by Owner decision 2026-11-24-4)
**Refines / supersedes:** refines the retry rules of feature 002 (`RetryPolicy`, Decision 26 of `specs/002-durable-execution/research.md`) and the lease rules of `docs/architecture/execution-semantics.md`; refines [ADR-0089](ADR-0089-a-gated-step-waits-outside-ready-and-one-fenced-grant-routine-serves-every-waiter-by-key.md) decisions 4 and 5 (a waiter's deadline and eligibility gain the close deadline); keeps feature 037 and Architecture decision 0076 unchanged; composes with Architecture decisions 0098 and 0099 (lease withdrawal and delivery acknowledgement, not merged). Supersedes none.
**Enforced by:** to be built with design 045 (`docs/designs/045-activity-timeouts.md`, Detailed Implementation Plan): the unit tests of the retry decision table (root, scoped and path steps) and of the deadline arithmetic in `crates/domain`; the replay-equals-projection test comparing the deadline columns; the barrier tests of a claim, a completion, a withdrawal, a gate, a pause, a reconciliation and an operator's retry against each deadline (`tests/fault/activity-timeouts/`, `fault_activity_timeouts`); the parity test of claim eligibility between the candidate query and the domain's lease decision; a rule in `scripts/check-architecture.py` that the deadline columns are assigned only from event values; the parity test of the outcome-unknown codes (`crates/domain/tests/outcome_unknown_parity.rs`), which must pass unchanged; the claim budget lines of `integration_claim_transition_efficiency`, which must not grow; the registry tests of the two requirements and their replay floor; the conformance scenarios `activity-timeouts`, `retry-by-code` and `heartbeat-from-handler` in six languages.

## Context

A Temporal activity has four timeouts: schedule-to-start (a task waits in its queue), start-to-close (one attempt runs), schedule-to-close (all attempts and their waits) and heartbeat (an attempt stops making progress), and a retry policy whose `nonRetryableErrorTypes` makes some errors final. This platform has two of them. A node's `timeoutMs` is an attempt deadline fixed at the claim (`steps.attempt_deadline_at`, which no renewal extends), and the lease expiry (`steps.lease_expires_at`, 30 seconds by default for every node, `lease_ttl` in `crates/application/src/worker.rs`) is a heartbeat timeout that is the same for every activity. The retry policy (`crates/domain/src/workflow.rs`) has no rule per error: a worker's `retryable` flag is the only way to make a failure final.

Three facts of this platform shape the design and differ from Temporal:

- **An attempt that ends without its report has an unknown outcome** (feature 037, constitution principle III). `LEASE_EXPIRED` and `ATTEMPT_TIMEOUT` send a declared activity to reconciliation or to a person, never to an automatic retry (`end_attempt` in `crates/domain/src/execution/command.rs`). Temporal retries a timed-out activity because it assumes activities are idempotent; this platform does not assume it.
- **The worker already names its failure.** `FailStep.error_code` is a machine code that every SDK restricts to `[A-Z0-9_]{1,64}` (`ActivityFailure(code, message, retryable)`, `sdks/SPEC.md` §6.5). It is the error type.
- **Time is the database's.** A run transaction reads the database's time under the run lock (`db_now`); scans compare with the database's `now()`; a pass re-checks under the run lock before it acts (`lost_leases` in `crates/infra-postgres/src/maintenance.rs`, the passes of `crates/scheduler/src/passes.rs`).

## Decision

1. **Four timeouts, one meaning each, all optional, on `activity` and `script` nodes of the forward path, in the root graph and in bodies.** `timeoutMs` keeps its meaning: start-to-close of one attempt. `scheduleToStartMs` bounds the time from the moment an attempt becomes claimable (`READY`) to its lease. `scheduleToCloseMs` bounds the time from the step's first release to its end, every attempt, retry wait and gate wait included. `heartbeatTimeoutMs` (10,000 to 86,400,000 ms) replaces the platform's lease time-to-live for the leases of that node. Compensation and reconciliation nodes keep today's rules, and the close deadline does not bound a reconciliation: a deadline MUST NOT end a pending compensation, a reconciliation or a review (constitution principle III).
2. **Each deadline is computed by the domain from `db_now` when the transition that starts it happens, recorded in that transition's event, and written to its column from the event.** The start deadline is set when the attempt becomes claimable and kept through its lease and any withdrawal (the withdrawn lease never started). The close deadline is set at the step's first release, kept by every later attempt, and cleared when a reconciliation, a review, an operator's hold or an operator's retry takes over. An attempt's deadline at its claim MUST be the earlier of the claim's time plus `timeoutMs` and the close deadline, and the step records which one it is.
3. **The lease decision is the domain's and never leases a step past its deadline.** It refuses a step whose start deadline has passed, whose close deadline is less than the close floor away (the earlier of `timeoutMs` and 6,000 ms, one receive bound), or that needs a protocol minor the session lacks; the candidate query carries the same conditions as a hint, so unclaimable rows do not fill a poll's window. A pass of its own then ends the step under the run lock, re-checking the shard fence and the step. A pause suspends the start deadline (the resume moves it later by the paused time); it does not move the close deadline.
4. **A deadline that ends no live attempt is a known failure; a deadline that ends a live attempt is an unknown outcome.** `SCHEDULE_TO_START_TIMEOUT` and `SCHEDULE_TO_CLOSE_TIMEOUT` reached while no attempt is live end the step `FAILED` with that code, are not retried, and take the node's failure route; a worker cannot report either code (it is recorded `WORKER_ERROR`). A close deadline reached during an attempt ends that attempt as its attempt deadline: `ATTEMPT_TIMEOUT` with `deadline: scheduleToClose`, and the step goes to reconciliation or review as feature 037 says. A silent attempt ends `LEASE_EXPIRED` whatever its time-to-live, with `deadline: heartbeat` when the node declares `heartbeatTimeoutMs`; no outcome-unknown code is added.
5. **Retry is decided per failure code, by one function for root, scoped and path steps, after the rules that already come first.** The order MUST be: an outcome-unknown code goes to reconciliation or review; a step asked to stop is cancelled; a worker's `retryable = false` is final; a code in `retry.nonRetryableErrorCodes` is final; the effective policy is the base policy overlaid by the one entry of `retry.byErrorCode` that names the code, and is spent when the step's attempt count reaches its `maxAttempts`; another attempt is scheduled only when its time is before the close deadline less the close floor, and otherwise the step fails `SCHEDULE_TO_CLOSE_TIMEOUT` with the last attempt's code recorded. A code that the platform decides elsewhere MUST NOT appear in either list, and each effective policy MUST be valid; publication refuses both.
6. **A worker may ask for a longer delay before the next attempt.** `FailStep.next_retry_delay_ms` (protocol 1.9) replaces the computed delay, without jitter, for that one retry, clamped to at least the effective initial delay and at most 24 hours; the event records that the delay came from the worker; it does not add an attempt and it cannot pass the close deadline.
7. **A handler can be the source of the heartbeat.** With `heartbeatFrom: "handler"` (which requires `heartbeatTimeoutMs` and `scheduleToStartMs`) a host renews the lease only after its handler called `heartbeat()` since the last renewal, so a handler that stops making progress loses its lease, which is Temporal's meaning of a heartbeat. Such a node is leased only to a session at protocol 1.9 or later. With `"host"`, the default, the host renews while the handler runs, as today.
8. **The rules are switched on by the fleet, with a replay floor.** A version that uses any field of this decision publishes only once the operator recorded `activity-timeouts-v1`, and one that uses the handler source also `activity-timeouts-handler-v1`, which only a build that offers protocol 1.9 lists. Publication holds the requirement in its transaction through a definer function, so a removal cannot slip between. A requirement MUST NOT be removed while a run journal that is not purged holds a transition of this decision, because an older build cannot replay it. A version published before keeps today's behaviour for every run in flight.

## Considered options

1. **Deadlines computed by the domain, journaled, kept on step rows; a lease decision and a candidate query that respect them; retry per code on `error_code` (chosen).** No new statement on the claim or the completion, one pass with its own window, the outcome-unknown rule untouched, and every decision journaled.
2. **Temporal's semantics as they are: retry after every timeout.** Simpler to explain to a Temporal user, but it repeats effects whose outcome is unknown, which constitution principle III and feature 037 forbid. Rejected.
3. **Timers in the `timers` machinery (one timer row per deadline).** One more row and one more statement per attempt for every node that declares a timeout, on the hot path that design 038 measured at 38 statements per lease. Rejected for the cost; the step row already carries the attempt deadline.
4. **A new `HEARTBEAT_TIMEOUT` code.** It would mean what `LEASE_EXPIRED` means, grow the outcome-unknown list and its copies, and give every consumer two codes to match. Rejected after the zoom-out review of design 045; the `deadline` field says which bound passed.
5. **A new error-type field on `FailStep`.** Two names for one fact (`error_code` already is the type), a fork from what six SDKs send today. Rejected; the SDKs instead map exception classes to codes locally.
6. **Patterns or prefixes in `nonRetryableErrorCodes`.** Convenient, but a policy read by a person must say exactly which codes it names; exact codes keep the decision auditable. Left out of 0.3.0; addable later without breaking a version.
7. **The conditions only in the run transaction, or deadlines computed in SQL.** Rejected after the architecture and correctness reviews: the first lets unclaimable rows fill an older host's candidate window; the second gives a column two derivations, so the projection could differ from the replay.

## Consequences

- A step that cannot find a worker, or whose retries outlive their budget, ends by a rule the author chose instead of waiting forever or until its run deadline.
- A user who comes from Temporal sees a timed-out attempt wait for reconciliation or review instead of retrying. The design doc says so, names `reconcileWith` as the way to make it automatic, and the Studio shows the reason. A `script` node, which cannot declare `reconcileWith`, goes to review.
- Operational cost: four columns and two partial indexes on `steps`, built by reading the table once at the upgrade; one pass more per shard; a lease of a node with the minimum heartbeat timeout of 10 s beats at most three times as often as today (0.3 beats per second against 0.1); a node with a long heartbeat timeout holds a dead worker's step and its concurrency slot for that long; a lease under 18 s is never provisional under protocol 1.8, so it does not get delivery acknowledgement's protection.
- The outcome-unknown list and its copies do not change. Two known-failure codes are added.
- Rollback: before a requirement is recorded, nothing uses the fields; after, it can be removed only when no published version uses them and no unpurged journal holds their transitions.
