# ADR-0110: The timeouts of a leased step are deadlines on the database clock, only a deadline that ends a live attempt leaves its outcome unknown, and retry is decided per failure code

**Status:** Proposed
**Decided by:** agent, proposing for design 045 under the owner's delegation (Owner decision 2026-11-20-6, widened by Owner decision 2026-11-24-4)
**Refines / supersedes:** refines the retry rules of feature 002 (`RetryPolicy`, Decision 26 of `specs/002-durable-execution/research.md`) and the lease rules of `docs/architecture/execution-semantics.md`; keeps feature 037 and Architecture decision 0076 unchanged; composes with Architecture decision 0098 and Architecture decision 0099 (lease withdrawal and delivery acknowledgement) and Architecture decision 0089 (a gated step waits outside `READY`). Supersedes none.
**Enforced by:** to be built with design 045 (`docs/designs/045-activity-timeouts.md`, Detailed Implementation Plan): the unit tests of the retry decision table and of the deadline arithmetic in `crates/domain`; the replay-equals-projection test of the new event fields; the barrier tests of a claim against the start deadline, of a completion against each deadline and of a retry against the close deadline (`tests/fault/activity-timeouts/`, `fault_activity_timeouts`); the parity test of the outcome-unknown codes (`crates/domain/tests/outcome_unknown_parity.rs`); the claim budget lines of `integration_claim_transition_efficiency`, which must not grow; the conformance scenarios `activity-timeouts` and `retry-by-code` in six languages.

## Context

A Temporal activity has four timeouts: schedule-to-start (a task waits in its queue), start-to-close (one attempt runs), schedule-to-close (all attempts and their waits) and heartbeat (an attempt stops making progress), and a retry policy whose `nonRetryableErrorTypes` makes some errors final. This platform has two of them. A node's `timeoutMs` is an attempt deadline fixed at the claim (`steps.attempt_deadline_at`, which no renewal extends), and the lease expiry (`steps.lease_expires_at`, 30 seconds by default for every node, `lease_ttl` in `crates/application/src/worker.rs`) is a heartbeat timeout that is the same for every activity. The retry policy (`crates/domain/src/workflow.rs`) has no rule per error: a worker's `retryable` flag is the only way to make a failure final.

Three facts of this platform shape the design and differ from Temporal:

- **An attempt that ends without its report has an unknown outcome** (feature 037, constitution principle III). `LEASE_EXPIRED` and `ATTEMPT_TIMEOUT` send a declared activity to reconciliation or to a person, never to an automatic retry (`end_attempt` in `crates/domain/src/execution/command.rs`). Temporal retries a timed-out activity because it assumes activities are idempotent; this platform does not assume it.
- **The worker already names its failure.** `FailStep.error_code` is a machine code of at most 64 characters that the handler chooses (`ActivityFailure(code, message, retryable)`, `sdks/SPEC.md` §6.5). It is the error type.
- **Time is the database's.** Every deadline today is written from the transition's time and compared with the database's `now()` by a scheduler pass that re-reads it under the run lock (`lost_leases` in `crates/infra-postgres/src/maintenance.rs`, `holds` in `crates/scheduler/src/passes.rs`).

## Decision

1. **Four timeouts, one meaning each, all optional, all on `activity` and `script` nodes of the forward path.** `timeoutMs` keeps its meaning: start-to-close of one attempt. `scheduleToStartMs` bounds the time from the moment an attempt becomes claimable (`READY`) to its lease. `scheduleToCloseMs` bounds the time from the step's first release to its end, every attempt, retry wait and gate wait included. `heartbeatTimeoutMs` replaces the platform's lease time-to-live for the leases of that node. Compensation and reconciliation nodes keep today's rules: a deadline MUST NOT end a pending compensation or its review (constitution principle III).
2. **Each deadline is written by the transition that starts it, from that transition's time, and compared only with the database's `now()`.** The start deadline is written when the attempt enters `READY` (the step's release, a retry that is due, a gate's grant) and is not moved by a lease withdrawal (Architecture decision 0098 decision 3), because the withdrawn lease never started. The close deadline is written at the step's first release and carried by every later attempt. An attempt's deadline at its claim MUST be the earlier of the claim's time plus `timeoutMs` and the close deadline, and the step row records which one it is.
3. **A claim never leases an attempt whose start deadline has passed.** The claim's condition includes `start_deadline_at > now()` on the database clock; a pass then ends the step with `SCHEDULE_TO_START_TIMEOUT` under the run lock, re-checking the deadline and the attempt's generation. The claim and the pass take the run lock, so exactly one of a lease and a timeout commits, and the deadline, not the order of arrival, decides which may.
4. **A deadline that ends no live attempt is a known failure; a deadline that ends a live attempt is an unknown outcome.** `SCHEDULE_TO_START_TIMEOUT` (no lease was ever committed for the attempt) and `SCHEDULE_TO_CLOSE_TIMEOUT` reached while no attempt is live (`READY`, `RETRY_WAIT`, waiting at a gate) end the step `FAILED` with that code, are not retried, and take the node's failure route. A close deadline reached during an attempt ends that attempt as its attempt deadline: `ATTEMPT_TIMEOUT`, whose event records `deadline: scheduleToClose`, and the step goes to reconciliation or review exactly as feature 037 says. A node that declares `heartbeatTimeoutMs` ends a silent attempt with `HEARTBEAT_TIMEOUT`, which joins the outcome-unknown codes; a node that does not keeps `LEASE_EXPIRED`.
5. **Retry is decided per failure code, after the rules that already come first.** The order MUST be: an outcome-unknown code goes to reconciliation or review; a step asked to stop is cancelled; a worker's `retryable = false` is final; a code in `retry.nonRetryableErrorCodes` is final; otherwise the policy is the base policy overlaid by the one entry of `retry.byErrorCode` that names the code, if any; another attempt is scheduled only while the attempt count is below that policy's `maxAttempts` and the next attempt's time comes before the close deadline; when it would not, the step fails with `SCHEDULE_TO_CLOSE_TIMEOUT` and the event records the last attempt's code. A code that the platform decides elsewhere (the outcome-unknown codes, `CANCELLED`, `TASK_NOT_STARTED`, the two schedule timeouts) MUST NOT appear in either list; publication refuses it.
6. **A worker may ask for the delay before the next attempt.** `FailStep.next_retry_delay_ms` (protocol 1.9) replaces the computed delay, without jitter, for that one retry, bounded to 24 hours; it does not add an attempt and it cannot pass the close deadline.
7. **A handler can be the source of the heartbeat.** With `heartbeatFrom: "handler"` a host renews the lease only after its handler called `heartbeat()` since the last renewal, so a handler that stops making progress loses its lease after `heartbeatTimeoutMs`, which is Temporal's meaning of a heartbeat. Such a node is leased only to a session at protocol 1.9 or later. With `"host"`, the default, the host renews while the handler runs, as today, and the per-node time-to-live only changes how fast a lost process is detected.
8. **The rules are switched on by the fleet, not per process.** A version that uses any field of this decision publishes only once the operator recorded the requirement `activity-timeouts-v1` (Architecture decision 0082); until then publication refuses it, and once recorded a binary without the capability serves nothing. A version published before keeps today's behaviour for every run in flight.

## Considered options

1. **Deadlines on step rows, a claim that respects the start deadline, retry per code on `error_code` (chosen).** No new statement on the claim or the completion, one scan per deadline on a partial index like the existing lost-lease scans, the outcome-unknown rule untouched, and every decision journaled.
2. **Temporal's semantics as they are: retry after every timeout.** Simpler to explain to a Temporal user, but it repeats effects whose outcome is unknown, which constitution principle III and feature 037 forbid. Rejected.
3. **Timers in the `timers` machinery (one timer row per deadline).** One more row and one more statement per attempt for every node that declares a timeout, on the hot path that design 038 measured at 38 statements per lease. Rejected for the cost; the step row already carries the attempt deadline.
4. **A new error-type field on `FailStep`.** Two names for one fact (`error_code` already is the type), a fork from what six SDKs send today. Rejected; the SDKs instead map exception classes to codes locally.
5. **Patterns or prefixes in `nonRetryableErrorCodes`.** Convenient, but a policy read by a person must say exactly which codes it names; exact codes keep the decision auditable. Left out of 0.3.0; addable later without breaking a version.

## Consequences

- A step that cannot find a worker, or whose retries outlive their budget, ends by a rule the author chose instead of waiting forever or until its run deadline.
- A user who comes from Temporal sees a timed-out attempt wait for reconciliation or review instead of retrying. The design doc says so, names `reconcileWith` as the way to make it automatic, and the Studio shows the reason.
- Four columns and two partial indexes on `steps`; building the indexes scans the table once at the upgrade.
- The outcome-unknown list grows from seven codes to eight; its copies (the SQL literal of the chain-progress store, the Studio's guards) move together under the parity test.
- Rollback: before the requirement is recorded, nothing uses the fields; after, the requirement can be removed only when no published version uses them, which the registry's command refuses otherwise.
