# Runbook: batch operations (feature 018)

For the people who run the platform and for operators who apply one action to many runs ("cancel everything stuck since Tuesday"). It says what a job does and promises, how to turn it on, how to read what became of each run, and what to look at when a job does not move.

Design: [ADR-0050](../adr/ADR-0050-many-run-operations-admit-under-stable-keys-and-never-buffer-or-reopen.md); decisions, gaps and owner questions: [`specs/018-schedules-bulk-operations/research.md`](../../specs/018-schedules-bulk-operations/research.md). Schedules are in [`schedules.md`](schedules.md).

## 1. What a job is and what it promises

A batch job applies **one action to many runs, one run at a time**, at a rate, in the background. It is created from the run list in the Studio (*Batch operation…*, with the filters you set) or over REST (`POST /namespaces/{id}/batch-operations`, preview first at `…/preview`), and followed at `/batches`.

- **Actions**: `CANCEL`, `SIGNAL`, `UPDATE` (a declared Update, by `update: {name, args}`), `RERUN`, `RETRY_STEP`, `PAUSE`, `RESUME`, `MARK_MANUAL_REVIEW`. `REPIN` is refused with `422 BATCH_ACTION_UNAVAILABLE` in this build (it needs feature 019); the demo of the quickstart created an `UPDATE` job (`202`) and was refused a `REPIN` one (`422`, `details.reason` `BATCH_ACTION_UNAVAILABLE`).
- **Which runs**: a filter (status, workflow, version, origin, schedule, a created-after and created-before, and the prefix of the business id: a literal, case-sensitive, matched on the id the run carries and read through an index; [`business-ids.md`](business-ids.md#6-the-identifiers-a-schedule-makes-and-selecting-runs-by-prefix) says how) or a list of run ids. The job takes the runs that existed when it was created, in pages, after every transaction that was open then has ended, so a run committed by one of those is not missed. The reason `BATCH_SELECTOR_UNSUPPORTED` stays in the closed set (it grows only) and nothing in this build answers it: the `020` query selector is refused with `QUERY_UNSUPPORTED_OPERATOR`.
- **Whose authority**: the creator's, **read again at every item**. The permission to run batch jobs (`run:batch`: admin, operator) *and* the permission of the single action are needed to create the job and still needed when each item is applied. A creator who loses either one fails the job (`AUTHORITY_REVOKED`). A run the creator may not see is skipped (`HIDDEN_FROM_CREATOR`), never described.
- **Exactly the single action**: each item goes through the same use case a person would use for that run, under the key `batch:{job}:{run}`, so a repeat after a crash does nothing twice and the audit trail shows the same record as the single action.
- **What it does not promise**: a job is not a transaction. Cancelling a job stops what has not run; **what was already applied stays applied**. An effect and the record of its item commit in **one transaction**, the run's (O1951, decided by the owner: OD-2026-11-13-2): after a crash an item says exactly what happened to its run, and a cancel of the job or a lease that is lost after the engine looked rolls the effect back instead of letting it through. The engine can still repeat an effect of the key that an engine older than this one applied without recording its item: the repeat changes nothing and the item is then recorded.

## 2. Limits

| Limit | Default | Hard | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| runs in one job (`maxItems`) | 10,000 | 100,000 | the selection stops there |
| rate (`itemsPerSecond`) | 20 | 200 | one item per slot; a job that ran late never catches up in a burst; a ceiling, not what a job gets (section 2.1: one replica applies about 73 items a second to all its jobs) |
| active jobs (`batch_jobs`, per namespace) | 2 | per `quota_ceilings` | a job counts until it ends |
| active jobs (`tenant_batch_jobs`, per tenant) | 8 | per `quota_ceilings` | |
| retries of an item refused for now | 10 within 1 hour | 50 within 24 hours | quota, rate, lock wait, a full queue |
| reason | 8 to 2,000 characters | | plain text, no new line, kept with the job |
| how long a job is kept (`expiresAt`) | 30 days | 90 days | stamped on the job; nothing purges it yet (section 6) |

### 2.1 The rate a job really gets

The rate is a **ceiling, never a promise** (NFR-018-005 promises nothing: the figures below are measurements of one machine on one day, not a guarantee). A job applies at most one item per slot (`1 / itemsPerSecond`) and never catches up in a burst after it ran late; how many it applies is the lesser of that and what the replica that holds its shard can do:

- **A lone job at the default rate (20 a second)** applied **1.0 item a second before 2026-11-09** and applies **18.4 items a second** (18.3 on a machine at four times the load; the 1.6 missing are the idle round that follows each item (a clock read and the scan, which also says the slot ahead) and the wake-up of the loop, about 4 ms an item) after the change that makes the engine's loop wake when the next slot comes instead of sleeping the whole `SAGAT_BATCHES_POLL_MS` (G2043, D2116; the measurement is the addendum "After the fixes of 2026-11-09" of [the report](../../tests/performance/reports/schedules-bulk-operations.md)). The rate was bounded by the poll before: the executor applies at most one item of a job in a step, the next slot of a job is 50 ms after an item, and a loop that found nothing due slept a second.
- **One replica applies about 73 to 75 items a second to all its jobs together** (13 ms an item, 7.4 ms of it the action itself; release build, PostgreSQL in Docker, no worker, no HTTP), and shares them fairly: sixteen jobs of one size ended within 1% of each other, none waited more than 0.4 s for its first item at the default rate, and a job of 900 runs beside fifteen of 30 did not hold the small ones back. So **sixteen jobs at the default rate (320 a second asked) got about 4.5 items a second each**, and a job at the highest rate (200 a second) got about 75 a second: more than one replica can apply is not applied. A second replica holds other shards, so it applies the items of other jobs; the batch pass on two replicas was not measured (G2050).
- **To find how long a job will take**, divide its runs by `min(itemsPerSecond, 73 / the number of active jobs on the replica)`; 10,000 runs at the default rate are about 9 minutes alone (18.4 a second) and about 36 minutes among sixteen jobs (4.6 a second each). The selection of the runs (0.3 s for 10,000) and the drain of a cancelled job (1.3 s for 10,000 items) are never the limit.

## 3. Turning it on

Off by default, in two places that **must agree**. Turn it on in the engines first and then in the control plane, and turn it off in the control plane first: the same order as the switch of the reset of an open run (ADR-0081), so that every job that is accepted has an engine to run it. The registry that would refuse a mismatch is not built (ADR-0050 decision 9):

| Where | Setting | Default | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| control plane | `SAGAT_BATCHES_ENABLED` | `false` | serves the routes (otherwise `404 ROUTE_NOT_FOUND`) |
| engine | `SAGAT_BATCHES_ENABLED` | `false` | runs the jobs of the shards the replica holds |
| engine | `SAGAT_BATCHES_BATCH` | 100 (1 to 5,000) | due jobs looked at in a round |
| engine | `SAGAT_BATCHES_ROUND_ACTIONS` | 100 (1 to 1,000) | steps one round takes over all its jobs |
| engine | `SAGAT_BATCHES_POLL_MS` | 1000 | pause when a round found nothing; the scan of the due jobs also says when the soonest next slot of a running job of the shards the replica holds comes, and the loop wakes then if that is sooner, and a due job that the executor finds not due yet (its clock reads a hair before the database's) is looked at again in 10 ms (G2043, section 2.1) |

With the routes on and no engine running jobs, a job is accepted and stays `SELECTING`, holding its place against the active-job limit, until an engine with the flag runs it; a cancellation waits for one too (G1965). Turning the flag off in the control plane stops the routes, not the jobs already running: the engine's flag is the switch.

## 4. Reading a job

`GET /namespaces/{id}/batch-operations/{id}` gives the state and the counters; `…/items` the outcome of each run, with a reason, in pages and by outcome.

- **Job states**: `SELECTING` (finding the runs), `RUNNING`, `CANCELLING` (marking the runs that have not run), `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED`, `FAILED` (with `AUTHORITY_REVOKED`, or `SELECTION_FAILED` when the platform could not select the runs: section 5.1).
- **Item outcomes**: `SUCCEEDED`, `SKIPPED`, `FAILED`, `CANCELLED`, `DEFERRED` (refused for now, tried again with a growing delay), `PENDING`.
- **Why skipped**: `ALREADY_TERMINAL` (the run had ended), `NOT_APPLICABLE` (the action does not apply to the run's state), `NOT_WAITING` (no step was waiting for the signal), `OPERATION_NOT_DECLARED`, `RUN_CONTINUED` (the run was handed to a continuation), `HIDDEN_FROM_CREATOR`.
- **Why deferred**: `QUOTA_EXCEEDED`, `RATE_LIMITED`, `CONCURRENCY_QUEUE_FULL`, `LOCK_WAIT`. An item refused too often or for too long fails with the code of the last refusal (`errorCode`).
- The payload of a signal and the arguments of an update are charged content: a read says only that the job carries one, never its value or its digest.

## 5. When a job does not move

1. **`SELECTING` for a long time.** The selection waits for every transaction that was open when the job was created. Look in the database for a transaction that has been open a long time (`pg_stat_activity.xact_start`); ending it lets the job go on. This is not the scheduler being late and no replica added will change it, so `scheduler_lag_seconds{pass="batches"}` does not count it (G1963: the wait has no deadline).
2. **`RUNNING` and nothing changes.** Is `SAGAT_BATCHES_ENABLED` on in an engine, does a replica hold the job's shard, is `scheduler_lag_seconds{pass="batches"}` high? Is the rate low (`itemsPerSecond`) for the number of runs, or are many jobs sharing the replica's 73 items a second (section 2.1)? Are the items `DEFERRED`? (`QUOTA_EXCEEDED` means the platform is at the limit of what the action needs, for example active runs of a rerun.)
3. **Many `FAILED`.** The `errorCode` of each is the single action's own code; the same failure would have come from doing it by hand.
4. **`FAILED` with `AUTHORITY_REVOKED`.** The creator no longer holds a permission the action needs. Create the job again as somebody who does.
5. **A cancelled job still shows runs that were cancelled by it.** That is right: the item that ran stays `SUCCEEDED`, the ones that had not run become `CANCELLED`.
6. **`SELECTING` and the transaction wait is not the cause, or `FAILED` with `SELECTION_FAILED`.** The platform could not read the runs the selector reaches: section 5.1.

### 5.1 A selection that keeps failing (`SELECTION_FAILED`, G2093)

The selection reads a page of the runs the selector matches in one transaction with a ten-second statement timeout. A round can fail: the statement runs over its timeout (a selector that reaches most of a namespace, such as a business-id prefix that most ids start with, or a status filter over a very large namespace, costs about its matches for every page; G2089), a deadlock victim, a database that is overloaded or restarting, an error that comes again. A failed round rolls back its own work and is **recorded** in the job: `batch_jobs.selection_failures` goes up by one and the job waits before its next round, **5 s, 10, 20, 40, 80, 160, then 300 s** (the wait of an item that is refused, by the database's clock). **A round that picks a page puts the count back to zero**, so only failures in a row count, and one timeout on a loaded database costs the job five seconds. The job survives **ten** failed rounds in a row; **the eleventh ends it `FAILED` with `failureReason: SELECTION_FAILED`**, about 25 minutes after the first failure, in the same transaction that counts it (and so exactly once, across a restart or a move of the shard lease: the count is the database's, not the engine's).

- **What the person sees.** The job is `SELECTING` while it fails and waits (there is nothing to read of the failure until it ends: `GET …/batch-operations/{id}` has no count and no cause, and no error text of any kind), then `FAILED` with `SELECTION_FAILED`, `finishedAt`, and `counts` that add up (the runs its earlier pages had selected are items `CANCELLED`, none of their runs touched; no item was applied, because items are applied only once the selection ends). The Studio says "The platform could not select the runs" and the CLI prints `failed: SELECTION_FAILED`. What to do: narrow the selector (a longer prefix, a status, fewer runs: the preview says how many it reaches) or create the job in parts, and create it again; nothing the job did needs undoing.
- **What the operator sees.** `batch_selection_failures_total{outcome}` with two values: `retry` (a failed round was recorded and the job waits) and `failed` (a job ended `SELECTION_FAILED`), counted once the record is committed, no label that is a tenant, a job or a selector. One warning for each, with the id of the job, the number of failures in a row and the **SQLSTATE** of the error and nothing else (`a batch selection round failed; the job waits before the next one`, `a batch job failed: its selection rounds kept failing`): `sqlstate=57014` is a statement over its timeout (a selector too wide), `40P01` a deadlock, `none` an error that is no database error (a pool or a connection); the text of the error is never logged, because it can quote a value of the selector (the storage layer does not log it for a selection round either; it still does for other statements, G2094). The alert `SagatBatchSelectionFailed` fires for a quarter of an hour after a job ended `SELECTION_FAILED` (it is not silenced by other jobs running, which is why `SagatBatchJobsStalled` cannot tell this). Both series are on the page **at zero from the start of the engine**: Prometheus's `increase` does not count the first sample of a series, so a counter created by its first increment left the first job an engine ended unalerted (seen on the compose stack on 2026-11-11, G2102); an engine built before that change still misses the first one it ends, and the alert is right for every later one. **A burst of `outcome="failed"` across many jobs or tenants means the database, not the selectors**: look at the database (load, locks, a failover, the statement timeouts of other work) before you tell people to narrow their selectors, because every job that was selecting when the database failed its rounds for about 25 minutes ends, and none can be resumed (G2100). The count is in the table: `SELECT id, state, selection_failures, next_slot_at FROM batch_jobs WHERE selection_failures > 0` (the job waits until `next_slot_at`; a job that failed keeps 11 as the evidence). The audit trail has `batch.finished` with the reason `SELECTION_FAILED`, by the engine, once.
- **What it costs.** A selector that can never succeed costs the database at most eleven statements of ten seconds in about 25 minutes, and a job that waits is not read by the scan of the due jobs at all (so it holds no round back from the jobs behind it). A job that waits is found within one poll of its time. **Noisy neighbour (G2099, not built)**: the pass steps due jobs one after another and a failing round costs up to ten seconds, so a tenant at its active-job limits (two jobs a namespace, eight a tenant by default) with selectors that scan without an index (a status or origin filter that matches nothing) can take about 80 seconds of the loop's time in the first minutes of its waits, and the jobs of other tenants on the same shards wait behind it; after the eleventh failure the tenant can create them again. If one tenant's `outcome="retry"` dominates the series, look at its jobs. To see whether the selector is the cause, preview it (`POST …/batch-operations/preview`: five-second timeout, and how many runs it reaches); to stop a job that waits, cancel it: the cancel does not wait for the backoff.
- **Rolling out and rolling back.** Apply the migration `20270116000000_batch_selection_failures` before the engine that counts. An engine built before it never counts and goes on retrying for ever, as it did; an engine built with it, run against a schema without it, selects nothing until the migration is applied (no job ends by it). **Mixed versions**: an older engine that picks a page of a job does not reset the count, so after a rolling deploy a job that is still selecting can carry a count it should not and fail up to ten rounds early; accepted. **Rolling back**: the migration's `down` refuses while a job that is *still selecting* has failed rounds on record (`SELECT id FROM batch_jobs WHERE state = 'SELECTING' AND selection_failures > 0`): it would lose its progress towards the bound. Let those jobs end (at most about 25 minutes from their first failed round) or cancel them, then roll back; a job that ended `SELECTION_FAILED` does not stand in the way (its count, 11, goes with the column; its `failureReason` stays). A rollback leaves every job and no count.

| Signal | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `batch_jobs_active` | jobs that are selecting, running or being cancelled |
| `batch_items_total{action,outcome}` | what became of the items, counted once the outcome is committed |
| `batch_item_duration_seconds{action}` | how long applying the action to one run took |
| `batch_selection_failures_total{outcome}` | failed selection rounds (both series at zero from the start of the engine), counted once the record is committed: `retry` (the job waits and tries again) and `failed` (the job ended `SELECTION_FAILED`); section 5.1 |
| `scheduler_lag_seconds{pass="batches"}` | how long the oldest job the pass would take has waited; `SagatSchedulerLate` takes the maximum over every pass |
| `scheduler_fenced_total{kind="batch"}` | steps another replica or a lost lease took away |
| `SagatBatchSelectionFailed` (alert) | a job ended `SELECTION_FAILED` in the last quarter of an hour (it is not silenced by other jobs running): section 5.1; a burst of them is the database |
| `SagatBatchJobsStalled` (alert) | jobs are active and no run has had an outcome from any of them for fifteen minutes: look first at the two flags (section 3), then at an old open transaction (point 1 above), then at a selection that keeps failing (section 5.1: `batch_selection_failures_total`) |

No label is a tenant, a job or a run. The audit trail records `batch.created`, `batch.cancelled` and `batch.finished`, and each item's effect is audited as the single action is.

## 6. What is not built yet

- The purge of finished jobs and their items (T018-051, waiting on feature 020's selector).
- `REPIN` (feature 019) as a batch action. (`UPDATE` is built: each item applies the declared Update under the key of the item, `SUCCEEDED` when applied, `FAILED` with the `Error.code` of `updateRun` when a rule says no, `SKIPPED` with `ALREADY_TERMINAL`, `RUN_CONTINUED` or `OPERATION_NOT_DECLARED` otherwise.)
- A bounded wait on locks inside an item (G1958) and a deadline for the wait of the selection (G1963); the clock skew between the host that stamps `runs.created_at` and the database clock the selection uses (G1962).
- Keyset paging of the selection by (business id, run id), which would make a page of a wide selector cost its own size (G2089); the bound of section 5.1 limits the cost of a selection that cannot succeed, it does not remove the cause (G2096). A count in the read of a job (G2097); a job that waits is found within one poll of its time, not at it (G2095); a limit on what one tenant's failing selections cost the loop (G2099); a way to retry a job that failed its selection (G2100).
